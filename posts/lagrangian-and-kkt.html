The Lagrangian and KKT conditions

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
linear-algebraoptimizationlagrangianconstrained-optimization

Builds on: Gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians, Convexity and Jensen's inequality · Leads to: Primal and dual problems

At a constrained minimum you can't slide along the constraint and go lower. That forces $f$'s contour to touch the constraint, and the Lagrangian turns this into "set a gradient to zero".

Gradients are normal to level sets

Walking along a contour doesn't change $f$, so the slope along it is zero: $\nabla f$ is perpendicular to the contour.

$$v \text{ tangent to } \{f=c\} \ \Rightarrow\ \nabla f(x)^Tv=0.$$

Example

$f=x^2+y^2$, contour $f=4$ (a circle). At $(2,0)$: $\nabla f=(4,0)$, tangent $(0,1)$, dot product $0$. ✓

One equality constraint: tangency

At the optimum, sliding along the constraint can't change $f$ to first order. So $\nabla f$ and $\nabla g$ are normal to the same surface: they are parallel.

$$\min_x f(x)\ \text{ s.t. } g(x)=0: \qquad \nabla f(x^*)+\lambda\,\nabla g(x^*)=0 \ \text{ for some } \lambda\in\mathbb{R} \qquad (\text{if } \nabla g(x^*)\ne0).$$

Example

The Lagrangian

Fold each constraint into the objective with its own multiplier. Asking $L$ to be flat in every variable gives tangency and feasibility at once.

$$L(x,\lambda)=f(x)+\lambda^Tg(x), \qquad \nabla_xL=\underbrace{\nabla f}_{n\times1}+\underbrace{J_g^T}_{n\times m}\underbrace{\lambda}_{m\times1}=0, \qquad \nabla_\lambda L=g(x)=0.$$

$J_g^T\lambda=\sum_i\lambda_i\nabla g_i$: with $m$ constraints, $\nabla f$ must lie in the span of the constraint normals.

Example

$$L=x^2+y^2+\lambda(x+y-1):$$ $$2x+\lambda=0,\ \ 2y+\lambda=0,\ \ x+y=1 \ \Rightarrow\ x=y=\tfrac12,\ \lambda=-1.$$

Inequalities: the KKT conditions

An inequality is either slack (ignore it) or tight (treat it as an equality). A wall can only push you back toward the feasible side, so its multiplier is $\mu\ge0$.

$$\min_x f(x)\ \text{ s.t. } h_j(x)\le0\ (p \text{ of them}),\ \ g_i(x)=0\ (m \text{ of them}):$$

Stationarity$\underbrace{\nabla f}_{n\times1}+\underbrace{J_h^T}_{n\times p}\underbrace{\mu}_{p\times1}+\underbrace{J_g^T}_{n\times m}\underbrace{\lambda}_{m\times1}=0$
Primal feasibility$h_j(x^*)\le0,\ \ g_i(x^*)=0$
Dual feasibility$\mu_j\ge0$
Complementary slackness$\mu_j\,h_j(x^*)=0$: a slack constraint gets $\mu_j=0$

Necessary at a minimum (under mild regularity). For convex $f,h_j$ and affine $g_i$ they are also sufficient: any KKT point is a global minimum.

Example

$\min x^2+y^2$ s.t. $h=1-x-y\le0$. The unconstrained minimum $(0,0)$ has $h=1\gt0$: infeasible, so the wall is tight. At $x^*=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$:

$$\underbrace{(1,1)}_{\nabla f}+\mu\,\underbrace{(-1,-1)}_{\nabla h}=0 \ \Rightarrow\ \mu=1\ge0, \qquad \mu\,h(x^*)=1\cdot0=0. \ \checkmark$$

Derive it yourself

Project the origin onto a half-space: minimize $\tfrac12\|x\|^2$ s.t. $a^Tx\ge b$, with $b\gt0$, $a\ne0$. Use KKT to find $x^*$ and $\mu$.

Solution

$h=b-a^Tx\le0$. At $x=0$, $h=b\gt0$: infeasible, so the constraint is tight. Stationarity: $x-\mu a=0$, so $x=\mu a$. Tight: $a^Tx=\mu\|a\|^2=b$, so $\mu=b/\|a\|^2\ge0$ ✓ and $x^*=\dfrac{b}{\|a\|^2}\,a$. With $a=(1,1)$, $b=1$: $x^*=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, matching the example ($\mu=\tfrac12$ because $f$ is halved).
