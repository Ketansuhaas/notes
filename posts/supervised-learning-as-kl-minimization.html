Supervised learning as KL minimization

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
information-theorydeep-learningsupervised-learningkl-divergencemaximum-likelihoodcross-entropy

Builds on: Entropy and cross-entropy, KL divergence, Common distributions · Leads to: Generative modeling, Backpropagation via JVPs

Pick an output distribution, minimize forward KL to the data. The loss falls out: Gaussian gives MSE, Categorical gives cross-entropy.

The network outputs a distribution

"Predicts $y$" really means "predicts the parameters of a distribution over $y$".

$$(x,y)\sim p_{\text{data}}, \qquad \text{model: } p_\theta(y\mid x).$$

Regression$p_\theta(y\mid x)=\mathcal N\big(y;\,f_\theta(x),\,\sigma^2\big)$output = the mean
Classification$p_\theta(y\mid x)=\text{Cat}\big(y;\,\operatorname{softmax}(\underbrace{z}_{K\times1})\big)$output = $K$ logits

Forward KL = maximum likelihood

Pull $p_\theta(y\mid x)$ toward $p_{\text{data}}(y\mid x)$ at every $x$. The data's entropy has no $\theta$, so only the log-likelihood term matters.

Step 1: split the KL at one $x$ (definition of KL, then linearity of $\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}$):

$$\mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\text{data}}(\cdot\mid x)\,\|\,p_\theta(\cdot\mid x)\big)=\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}\Big[\log\frac{p_{\text{data}}(y\mid x)}{p_\theta(y\mid x)}\Big]$$

$$=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}\big[\log p_{\text{data}}(y\mid x)\big]}_{-H(y\mid x{=}x),\ \text{no }\theta}-\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}\big[\log p_\theta(y\mid x)\big].$$

Step 2: average over $x$. $\mathbb{E}_x\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}=\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim p_{\text{data}}}$:

$$\mathbb{E}_x\,\mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\text{data}}(y\mid x)\,\|\,p_\theta(y\mid x)\big)=\underbrace{-H(y\mid x)}_{\text{no }\theta}-\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim p_{\text{data}}}\big[\log p_\theta(y\mid x)\big].$$

Step 3: replace $\mathbb{E}$ by the training average. For i.i.d. pairs, $\frac1N\sum_i\log p_\theta(y_i\mid x_i)\to\mathbb{E}[\log p_\theta(y\mid x)]$ (LLN). So minimize

$$\underbrace{-\frac1N\sum_{i=1}^N\log p_\theta(y_i\mid x_i)}_{\text{NLL}}=-\frac1N\log\prod_{i=1}^Np_\theta(y_i\mid x_i).$$

$\log$ is increasing, so minimizing the NLL is maximizing the likelihood $\prod_ip_\theta(y_i\mid x_i)$ of the training set: forward KL $=$ maximum likelihood.

Gaussian $\to$ MSE

Assume $y$ is Gaussian around the prediction, and squared error is what MLE hands you.

Take $-\log$ of the density $\mathcal N(y;f,\sigma^2)=\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}\exp\!\big(-\frac{(y-f)^2}{2\sigma^2}\big)$; $\log$ undoes $\exp$:

$$-\log\mathcal N\big(y;f_\theta(x),\sigma^2\big)=\frac{(y-f_\theta(x))^2}{2\sigma^2}+\underbrace{\tfrac12\log(2\pi\sigma^2)}_{\text{const}}.$$

Average over the data. With $\sigma$ fixed, the factor $\frac1{2\sigma^2}\gt0$ and the constant do not move the minimizer:

$$\text{NLL}=\frac1{2\sigma^2}\cdot\frac1N\sum_i\big(y_i-f_\theta(x_i)\big)^2+\text{const} \quad\Rightarrow\quad \min_\theta\ \text{MSE}.$$

Example

$\sigma=1$, $y=3$, $f_\theta(x)=2.5$:

$$\frac{(3-2.5)^2}{2\cdot1}=\frac{0.25}2=0.125, \qquad \text{const}=\tfrac12\ln(2\pi)=0.919, \qquad \text{NLL}=1.044 \text{ nats}.$$

Categorical $\to$ cross-entropy

The NLL of the true class is exactly cross-entropy against the one-hot label.

A categorical with probabilities $\pi=\operatorname{softmax}(z)$ assigns $p(y{=}k)=\pi_k=\prod_j\pi_j^{[k=j]}$. Take $-\log$:

$$-\log p_\theta(y{=}k\mid x)=-\sum_j[j{=}k]\log\pi_j=H\big(e_k,\,\pi\big)=-\log\pi_k,$$

the one-hot cross-entropy from the entropy note. With $\pi_k=e^{z_k}/\sum_je^{z_j}$ it becomes

$$-\log\operatorname{softmax}(z)_k=-z_k+\log\sum_je^{z_j}.$$

Example

$z=(2,1,0)$, true class $1$ (the logit $2$). With $e^2=7.389$, $e^1=2.718$, $e^0=1$:

$$\operatorname{softmax}(z)_1=\frac{7.389}{7.389+2.718+1}=\frac{7.389}{11.107}=0.665, \qquad -\ln0.665=0.41.$$

Same from the logit form: $-2+\ln11.107=-2+2.408=0.41$. ✓

Both losses learn a mean

At the optimum, the output equals the average of what it is built to predict.

Assume the model is flexible enough to pick its output freely at each $x$; then the expected loss $\mathbb{E}_x\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}[\cdots]$ is minimized separately at each $x$.

MSE. Let $\bar y=\mathbb{E}[y\mid x]$ and $m$ the prediction. Split $y-m=(y-\bar y)+(\bar y-m)$ and square; the cross term has $\mathbb{E}[y-\bar y\mid x]=0$:

$$\mathbb{E}\big[(y-m)^2\mid x\big]=\underbrace{\operatorname{Var}(y\mid x)}_{\text{no }m}+(\bar y-m)^2.$$

Minimized at $m=\bar y$, so $f_\theta(x)\to\mathbb{E}[y\mid x]$, and the leftover loss is the noise $\operatorname{Var}(y\mid x)$. (Same as setting $\frac{d}{dm}\mathbb{E}[(y-m)^2]=-2\,\mathbb{E}[y-m]=0$.)

Cross-entropy. Average $-\log\pi_y$ over $y\mid x$:

$$\mathbb{E}_{y\mid x}[-\log\pi_y]=-\sum_kp_{\text{data}}(k\mid x)\log\pi_k=H\big(p_{\text{data}}(\cdot\mid x),\,\pi\big),$$

minimized at $\pi=p_{\text{data}}(\cdot\mid x)$ (exercise). That is a mean too: coordinate $k$ of $\mathbb{E}[e_y\mid x]$ is $P(y{=}k\mid x)$.

MSE$f_\theta(x)\to\mathbb{E}[y\mid x]$
Cross-entropy$\operatorname{softmax}(z)\to\mathbb{E}[e_y\mid x]=p_{\text{data}}(\cdot\mid x)$

Derive it yourself

Minimize $-\sum_kp_k\log q_k$ over the simplex $\sum_kq_k=1$. Show $q^\star=p$.

Solution

Add a multiplier $\nu$ for the constraint (see the Lagrangian):

$$L=-\sum_kp_k\log q_k+\nu\Big(\sum_kq_k-1\Big).$$

$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_k}=-\frac{p_k}{q_k}+\nu=0 \ \Rightarrow\ q_k=\frac{p_k}\nu.$$

$$\text{Constraint: } 1=\sum_kq_k=\frac{\sum_kp_k}\nu=\frac1\nu \ \Rightarrow\ \nu=1,\quad q^\star=p.$$

It is a minimum: each $-p_k\log q_k$ is convex in $q_k$, and the constraint is linear. (Or, without calculus: $H(p,q)=H(p)+\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)$, and $\mathrm{KL}\ge0$ with equality only at $q=p$.)
