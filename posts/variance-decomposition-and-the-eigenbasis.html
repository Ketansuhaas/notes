Variance decomposition and the eigenbasis

The covariance note gave the spread along one ruler. Here: turn the ruler all the way round, and ask what a random ruler gets.

The variance profile

As the ruler turns, its spread traces a smooth wave between $\lambda_{\min}$ and $\lambda_{\max}$. Its weights are squared cosines to the axes.

$$\operatorname{Var}(u^Tx)=\sum_i\lambda_i\cos^2\theta_i,\quad \textstyle\sum_i\cos^2\theta_i=1. \qquad d=2:\ \ \lambda_1\cos^2\theta+\lambda_2\sin^2\theta.$$

Example

$u=(1,0)$ sits at $45^\circ$: $3\cdot\tfrac12+1\cdot\tfrac12=2=C_{11}$. ✓

A random ruler gets the average

Point the ruler at random and you capture the mean eigenvalue. PCA's payoff is how far $\lambda_1$ sits above that.

$$u \text{ uniform on the unit sphere}: \qquad \mathbb{E}_u\big[u^TCu\big]=\frac{\operatorname{tr}C}{d}=\frac1d\sum_i\lambda_i.$$

Example

Average of the wave: $\tfrac{3+1}{2}=2$. PC1 gets $3$, i.e. $1.5\times$ a random direction. In $d=1000$ with one dominant axis, the gap is enormous.

Derive it yourself

Prove $\mathbb{E}_u[u^TCu]=\operatorname{tr}C/d$ for $u$ uniform on the unit sphere.

Solution

$u^TCu=\operatorname{tr}(Cuu^T)$, so $\mathbb{E}[u^TCu]=\operatorname{tr}(C\,\mathbb{E}[uu^T])$. By symmetry $\mathbb{E}[u_iu_j]=0$ for $i\ne j$ and all $\mathbb{E}[u_i^2]$ are equal; they sum to $\mathbb{E}\|u\|^2=1$. So $\mathbb{E}[uu^T]=I/d$, giving $\operatorname{tr}C/d$.
