A float is scientific notation in binary. 16-bit floats halve the bytes and unlock tensor cores; the art is choosing which bits to give up.

Sign, exponent, mantissa

The exponent is the "$\times2^e$" part: how big or tiny a number can be (range). The mantissa is the "$1.xxx$" part: how many digits it keeps (precision).

$$x=(-1)^s\times1.m\times2^{\,e-\text{bias}}.$$

biaslargestspacing at 1
fp32127$\approx3.4\times10^{38}$$2^{-23}\approx1.2\times10^{-7}$
bf16127$\approx3.4\times10^{38}$$2^{-7}\approx7.8\times10^{-3}$
fp1615$65504$$2^{-10}\approx9.8\times10^{-4}$

Example

bf16 keeps under 3 significant digits: $256+1=256$, because the spacing at 256 is $2^8\cdot2^{-7}=2$.

Why bf16 for training

Activations and gradients span many orders of magnitude. bf16 has fp32's range, so nothing overflows or flushes to zero; fp16's narrow range does both.

fp16above 65504 → inf; tiny gradients → 0needs loss scaling
bf16same range as fp32no scaling; coarse precision is tolerated

Loss scaling: multiply the loss by $S$ (e.g. $2^{16}$) so every gradient is $S\times$ larger, then divide by $S$ in fp32 before the update. Lower $S$ whenever an inf appears.

Mixed precision: low precision where it is cheap

Do the bulk math (matmuls) in bf16 on tensor cores; keep anything that adds up many small numbers in fp32.

matmul inputsbf16
matmul accumulationfp32 (inside the tensor core)
master weights, optimizer statefp32
softmax, norms, lossfp32 (autocast chooses this)

Example: why fp32 master weights

$w=1$, update $\eta g=10^{-6}$. In bf16, $1+10^{-6}=1$ (spacing $2^{-7}$): training silently stalls. In fp32 the update survives.

import torch, torch.nn.functional as F
model = Net().cuda()                                # weights stay fp32
opt = torch.optim.AdamW(model.parameters(), lr=1e-3)
for x, y in loader:
    x, y = x.cuda(), y.cuda()
    with torch.autocast("cuda", dtype=torch.bfloat16):
        loss = F.cross_entropy(model(x), y)         # matmuls run in bf16
    loss.backward()                                 # grads are fp32, like the weights
    opt.step(); opt.zero_grad()

For fp16, add scaler = torch.amp.GradScaler("cuda") and use scaler.scale(loss).backward(), scaler.step(opt), scaler.update().

Work it out yourself

What is the smallest positive normal number in fp16 and in bf16? A gradient of $10^{-8}$ is cast to fp16: what happens, and how does $S=2^{16}$ rescue it?

Solution

Smallest normal: exponent field $1$, mantissa $0$: $2^{1-\text{bias}}$. fp16: $2^{-14}\approx6.1\times10^{-5}$. bf16: $2^{-126}\approx1.2\times10^{-38}$.

Below that, fp16 has subnormals down to $2^{-24}\approx6\times10^{-8}$. $10^{-8}$ is under half of that, so it rounds to $0$: the gradient vanishes. In bf16 it is an ordinary number.

Scaled: $10^{-8}\cdot2^{16}\approx6.6\times10^{-4}$, a normal fp16 value. Unscale in fp32 and the update is intact.
