Momentum and Nesterov acceleration

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationmomentumnesterovconvergencecondition-number

Builds on: Gradient descent on strongly convex functions, Gradient descent on a quadratic, Dynamics in the eigenbasis · Leads to: Newton's method

Gradient descent forgets its last step. A heavy ball remembers it: its velocity keeps it rolling along the flat direction, and back-and-forth zig-zags cancel. On a quadratic this turns $\kappa$ into $\sqrt\kappa$.

The heavy-ball update

Take the gradient step, then add a fraction $\beta$ of the previous step.

$$x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\nabla f(x_k)+\beta\,(x_k-x_{k-1}),\qquad 0\le\beta\lt1.$$

Call the step $v_k=x_k-x_{k-1}$ (velocity, $v_0=0$). The update says $v_{k+1}=\beta v_k-\eta\nabla f(x_k)$; repeat it:

$$v_{k+1}=-\eta\big(\nabla f(x_k)+\beta\nabla f(x_{k-1})+\beta^2\nabla f(x_{k-2})+\cdots\big).$$

Example

Same gradient $g$ every step: the sum tends to $\frac{g}{1-\beta}$ (geometric series). Sign flipping every step ($g,-g,g,\dots$): it tends to $\frac{g}{1+\beta}$. With $\beta=0.9$: $10\,g$ versus $0.53\,g$. Steady directions speed up, zig-zags damp out.

One eigen-direction, two past values

On a quadratic, each eigen-direction is still its own 1-D problem, as for GD. It now remembers one extra step.

$f=\tfrac12x^TAx$, write $x_k=\sum_ic_k^{(i)}q_i$ in the eigenbasis, so $\nabla f=Ax_k=\sum_i\lambda_ic_k^{(i)}q_i$. Match the coefficient of one $q_i$ (drop the $i$):

$$c_{k+1}=c_k-\eta\lambda c_k+\beta(c_k-c_{k-1})=(1+\beta-\eta\lambda)\,c_k-\beta\,c_{k-1}.$$

Step 1: the characteristic equation

For GD the answer was $c_k=r^k$ with $r=1-\eta\lambda$. Try the same guess.

Substitute $c_k=r^k$ and divide by $r^{k-1}$:

$$r^2-(1+\beta-\eta\lambda)\,r+\beta=0.$$

With roots $r_1,r_2$, $c_k=a\,r_1^k+b\,r_2^k$, so this direction shrinks like $\max(|r_1|,|r_2|)^k$.

Step 2: complex roots all have size $\sqrt\beta$

The product of the roots is the constant term $\beta$, whatever $\lambda$ is. Complex roots come as a conjugate pair of equal size, so that size is $\sqrt\beta$.

$$r_1r_2=\beta,\quad r_2=\bar r_1\ \Rightarrow\ |r_1|^2=r_1\bar r_1=\beta\ \Rightarrow\ |r_1|=|r_2|=\sqrt\beta.$$

The roots are complex (or a double root) when the discriminant is $\le0$:

$$(1+\beta-\eta\lambda)^2\le4\beta\iff-2\sqrt\beta\le1+\beta-\eta\lambda\le2\sqrt\beta$$

$$\iff(1-\sqrt\beta)^2\le\eta\lambda\le(1+\sqrt\beta)^2.\qquad(1+\beta\mp2\sqrt\beta=(1\mp\sqrt\beta)^2)$$

Every direction whose $\eta\lambda$ lies in this window decays at the same rate $\sqrt\beta$.

Step 3: tune $\eta,\beta$ so the window is $[\mu,L]$

Put the window's ends exactly at the smallest and largest curvature; then every direction gets rate $\sqrt\beta$.

Set $\eta\mu=(1-\sqrt\beta)^2$, $\eta L=(1+\sqrt\beta)^2$ and take square roots:

$$\sqrt\eta\sqrt\mu=1-\sqrt\beta,\qquad \sqrt\eta\sqrt L=1+\sqrt\beta.$$

Add: $\sqrt\eta(\sqrt L+\sqrt\mu)=2$. Subtract: $\sqrt\eta(\sqrt L-\sqrt\mu)=2\sqrt\beta$. Divide the second by the first (then divide top and bottom by $\sqrt\mu$):

$$\eta=\frac{4}{(\sqrt L+\sqrt\mu)^2},\qquad \boxed{\sqrt\beta=\frac{\sqrt L-\sqrt\mu}{\sqrt L+\sqrt\mu}=\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}}$$

GD's best rate was $\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}$: the same formula with $\kappa$ replaced by $\sqrt\kappa$. At $\lambda=\mu$ and $\lambda=L$ the root is double, $c_k=(a+bk)r^k$: same rate, with an extra factor $k$.

Example: running example, $\kappa=3$

$$\sqrt\beta=\frac{\sqrt3-1}{\sqrt3+1}=\frac{(\sqrt3-1)^2}{3-1}=2-\sqrt3\approx0.268,\qquad \beta\approx0.072,$$

$$\eta=\frac{4}{(\sqrt3+1)^2}=\frac{4}{4+2\sqrt3}=2(2-\sqrt3)\approx0.536.$$

GD's best is $\frac{3-1}{3+1}=0.5$. Steps per digit, $\ln10/\ln\frac1{\text{rate}}$: $\ 2.30/1.32\approx1.75$ versus $2.30/0.69\approx3.3$.

Step 4: $\sqrt\kappa$ instead of $\kappa$

Steps needed $=\ln\frac1\varepsilon\,/\ln\frac1{\text{rate}}$, and $\ln\frac1{\text{rate}}$ is about $2/\sqrt\kappa$ instead of $2/\kappa$.

$$\ln\frac{\sqrt\kappa+1}{\sqrt\kappa-1}=\ln\Big(1+\tfrac1{\sqrt\kappa}\Big)-\ln\Big(1-\tfrac1{\sqrt\kappa}\Big)\approx\frac{2}{\sqrt\kappa}\qquad(\ln(1+u)\approx u)$$

$$\Rightarrow\ k\approx\frac{\sqrt\kappa}{2}\ln\frac1\varepsilon,\qquad\text{GD (same algebra): } k\approx\frac{\kappa}{2}\ln\frac1\varepsilon.$$

This $\sqrt\kappa$ proof is for quadratics only: Lessard, Recht and Packard (2016) found a smooth, strongly convex function on which the tuned heavy ball cycles forever. Nesterov's version below is provably accelerated beyond quadratics.

Nesterov: measure the gradient where you are going

Heavy ball takes the gradient at $x_k$, then coasts. Nesterov coasts first to a look-ahead point $y_k$ and takes the gradient there, so it corrects an overshoot one step earlier.

$$y_k=x_k+\beta_k(x_k-x_{k-1}),\qquad x_{k+1}=y_k-\tfrac1L\nabla f(y_k),\qquad y_0=x_0,$$

$$\beta_k=\frac{t_k-1}{t_{k+1}},\qquad t_1=1,\quad t_{k+1}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}}{2}\qquad(\beta_k\approx\tfrac{k-1}{k+2}\to1).$$

For $L$-smooth convex $f$ (no $\mu$ needed):

$$f(x_k)-f^*\le\frac{2L\,\|x_0-x^*\|^2}{(k+1)^2},$$

versus $\frac{L\|x_0-x^*\|^2}{2k}$ for GD. To get the gap below $10^{-4}L\|x_0-x^*\|^2$: GD needs $\frac1{2k}\le10^{-4}$, $k=5000$; Nesterov needs $(k+1)^2\ge2\cdot10^4$, $k=141$.

Proof idea. (1) Define an energy $E_k=t_k^2\,(f(x_k)-f^*)+\tfrac L2\|t_kx_k-(t_k-1)x_{k-1}-x^*\|^2$. (2) Show $E_{k+1}\le E_k$: the descent lemma at $y_k$ plus convexity at $x_k$ and at $x^*$, weighted $t_{k+1}-1$ and $1$; the cross terms cancel because $t_{k+1}$ solves $t^2-t=t_k^2$. This is the page of algebra a full proof needs (Beck and Teboulle 2009). (3) $\sqrt{1+4t_k^2}\ge2t_k$ gives $t_{k+1}\ge t_k+\tfrac12$, so $t_k\ge\frac{k+1}2$. (4) One GD step gives $E_1\le\tfrac L2\|x_0-x^*\|^2$, so

$$t_k^2(f(x_k)-f^*)\le E_k\le E_1\le\tfrac L2\|x_0-x^*\|^2\ \Rightarrow\ f(x_k)-f^*\le\frac{L\|x_0-x^*\|^2}{2t_k^2}\le\frac{2L\|x_0-x^*\|^2}{(k+1)^2}.$$

Derive it yourself

Write the one-direction recurrence $c_{k+1}=(1+\beta-\eta\lambda)c_k-\beta c_{k-1}$ as $z_{k+1}=Mz_k$ with $z_k=(c_k,c_{k-1})$. Show the eigenvalues of $M$ solve the characteristic equation, then find them for the tuned $\kappa=3$ parameters at $\lambda=1$, $2$, $3$.

Solution

The first row is the recurrence, the second row just copies $c_k$ down:

$$\begin{pmatrix}c_{k+1}\\c_k\end{pmatrix}=\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}1+\beta-\eta\lambda&-\beta\\1&0\end{pmatrix}}_{M}\begin{pmatrix}c_k\\c_{k-1}\end{pmatrix}.$$

$$\det(M-rI)=(1+\beta-\eta\lambda-r)(-r)+\beta=r^2-(1+\beta-\eta\lambda)r+\beta.$$

Same polynomial, so $z_k=M^kz_0$ shrinks like $\max|r|^k$ (dynamics in the eigenbasis). Also $\det M=\beta=r_1r_2$.

With $\eta=4-2\sqrt3$, $\beta=7-4\sqrt3$, the trace is $T=1+\beta-\eta\lambda$:

$\lambda=1$: $T=8-4\sqrt3-4+2\sqrt3=2(2-\sqrt3)$, and $T^2-4\beta=4(2-\sqrt3)^2-4(2-\sqrt3)^2=0$: double root $r=T/2=2-\sqrt3\approx0.268$.

$\lambda=3$: $T=8-4\sqrt3-12+6\sqrt3=-2(2-\sqrt3)$: double root $r=-(2-\sqrt3)$, size $0.268$ (the sign flip is a damped zig-zag).

$\lambda=2$: $T=8-4\sqrt3-8+4\sqrt3=0$, so $r^2=-\beta$, $r=\pm i(2-\sqrt3)$: complex, size $\sqrt\beta=0.268$. All three decay at the same rate. ✓
