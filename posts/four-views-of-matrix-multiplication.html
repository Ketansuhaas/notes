Four views of matrix multiplication

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
linear-algebramatrix-multiplication

Builds on: Reading the notation in papers · Leads to: Rank, subspaces, and projections, The covariance matrix, intuitively

One computation, four ways to read it. Each answers a different question.

$$ \underbrace{A}_{m\times n}\underbrace{B}_{n\times p}=\underbrace{AB}_{m\times p}, \qquad A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix},\ B=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&3\end{pmatrix},\ AB=\begin{pmatrix}3&3\\3&6\end{pmatrix}. $$

1. Entries are dot products

Entry $(i,j)$: how well row $i$ of $A$ lines up with column $j$ of $B$.

This is the definition; every other view is this one sum regrouped.

$$(AB)_{ij}:=\sum_{k=1}^nA_{ik}B_{kj}=\text{row}_i(A)\cdot\text{col}_j(B).$$

All four entries of the example:

$$(AB)_{11}=2\cdot1+1\cdot1=3,\quad (AB)_{12}=2\cdot0+1\cdot3=3,$$ $$(AB)_{21}=1\cdot1+2\cdot1=3,\quad (AB)_{22}=1\cdot0+2\cdot3=6.$$

2. Combinations of columns

Columns of $A$ are ingredients, $x$ is the recipe. $Ax$ can only make mixtures of those columns.

Take entry $i$ of $Ax$ and read $A_{ik}$ as entry $i$ of column $a_k$:

$$(Ax)_i=\sum_kA_{ik}x_k=\sum_kx_k\,(a_k)_i \quad\Rightarrow\quad Ax=x_1a_1+\cdots+x_na_n.$$

Column $j$ of $AB$ is $A$ times column $j$ of $B$ (fix $j$ in the definition), so each column of $AB$ is such a mix. Column 2, with $\text{col}_2(B)=(0,3)$:

$$A\begin{pmatrix}0\\3\end{pmatrix}=0\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\end{pmatrix}+3\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}3\\6\end{pmatrix}=\text{col}_2(AB). \ \checkmark$$

3. Combinations of rows

Each output row is its own computation. In $XW$ (data as rows), each data point is transformed alone.

Fix $i$ and read $B_{kj}$ as entry $j$ of row $k$ of $B$:

$$(AB)_{ij}=\sum_kA_{ik}\,\big(\text{row}_k(B)\big)_j \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{row}_i(AB)=\sum_kA_{ik}\,\text{row}_k(B)=\text{row}_i(A)\,B.$$

Row 1, with $\text{row}_1(A)=(2,1)$ as the weights:

$$\text{row}_1(AB)=2\,(1,0)+1\,(1,3)=(3,3). \ \checkmark$$

4. Sum of outer products

A product is a stack of rank-1 layers: column $k$ of $A$ times row $k$ of $B$.

Pull the sum over $k$ outside. Each term $A_{ik}B_{kj}$ is entry $(i,j)$ of the outer product $a_kb_k^T$ (column $a_k$ of $A$, row $b_k^T$ of $B$):

$$(AB)_{ij}=\sum_k\underbrace{A_{ik}B_{kj}}_{(a_kb_k^T)_{ij}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad AB=\sum_ka_kb_k^T.$$

Each layer has rank 1 because row $i$ of $a_kb_k^T$ is $(a_k)_i\,b_k^T$, a multiple of the single row $b_k^T$.

$$ AB=\sum_k\underbrace{a_k}_{m\times1}\underbrace{b_k^T}_{1\times p}:\quad \begin{pmatrix}2\\1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1\\2\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&3\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\1&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\2&6\end{pmatrix}=AB. $$

Which view, when

Dot productssimilarity, attention scores $QK^T$
Columnswhat $Ax$ can reach, column space
Rowsa batch of data, $XW$
Outer productsrank, SVD, covariance, weight gradients $\delta x^T$

Derive it yourself

Rows of $X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}$ are the data points $x_i^T$. Show $X^TX=\sum_ix_ix_i^T$.

Solution

View 4 with $A=X^T$ ($d\times n$) and $B=X$ ($n\times d$). Column $i$ of $X^T$ is $x_i$ (transposing turns row $i$ into column $i$), and row $i$ of $X$ is $x_i^T$, so

$$X^TX=\sum_{i=1}^n\underbrace{x_i}_{d\times1}\underbrace{x_i^T}_{1\times d}\qquad(d\times d).$$

Entry check, from the definition: with $X_{ij}=(x_i)_j$,

$$(X^TX)_{jl}=\sum_i(X^T)_{ji}X_{il}=\sum_i(x_i)_j(x_i)_l=\Big(\sum_ix_ix_i^T\Big)_{jl}. \ \checkmark$$
