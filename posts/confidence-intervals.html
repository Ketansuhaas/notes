Confidence intervals: the z and t statistics

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran

What "95%" claims

$\mu$ is fixed; the interval is random. "95%" describes the procedure: repeat it many times and 95% of the intervals catch $\mu$. Any one interval either hit or missed.

$$P\big(L(x_{1:n})\le\mu\le U(x_{1:n})\big)=1-\alpha.$$

The probability is over samples (random $L,U$), not over $\mu$.

$\sigma$ known: the z-interval

Estimate $\pm$ margin. The margin is the standard error $\sigma/\sqrt n$ times how many standard deviations the normal needs to hold $1-\alpha$ of its mass.

$$Z_n=\frac{\bar x_n-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\approx\mathcal N(0,1) \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \mu\in\bar x_n\pm z_{\alpha/2}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt n}, \qquad z_{0.025}=1.96.$$

Example

$n=100$, $\bar x=5.2$, $\sigma=2$: standard error $0.2$, so $\mu\in5.2\pm0.39=[4.81,\ 5.59]$.

$\sigma$ unknown: the t-interval

Plug in $\hat\sigma$ (divide by $n-1$). But $\hat\sigma$ is random too: when it comes out small, $T_n$ overshoots. So $T_n$ needs heavier tails than $\mathcal N(0,1)$.

$$T_n=\frac{\bar x_n-\mu}{\hat\sigma/\sqrt n}\sim t_{n-1} \ \ \Rightarrow\ \ \mu\in\bar x_n\pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}\frac{\hat\sigma}{\sqrt n}.$$

Exact if the $x_i$ are Gaussian; approximate otherwise (CLT). The $n-1$ is the same degrees of freedom left in $\hat\sigma$. As $n\to\infty$, $t_{n-1}\to\mathcal N(0,1)$.

$n$$5$$10$$30$$\infty$
$t_{n-1,\,0.025}$$2.78$$2.26$$2.05$$1.96$

Example

$n=10$, $\bar x=5.2$, $\hat\sigma=2$: standard error $0.632$. t-margin $2.26\cdot0.632=1.43$ vs. z-margin $1.24$. Not knowing $\sigma$ costs a wider interval.

Side by side

$\sigma$ known$\bar x_n\pm z_{\alpha/2}\,\sigma/\sqrt n$
$\sigma$ estimated$\bar x_n\pm t_{n-1,\alpha/2}\,\hat\sigma/\sqrt n$

Derive it yourself

Solve $-z\le Z_n\le z$ for $\mu$ to get the z-interval. Then find how large $n$ must be for a 95% half-width of at most $E$. Try $\sigma=2$, $E=0.1$.

Solution

Multiply by $\sigma/\sqrt n$: $-z\sigma/\sqrt n\le\bar x_n-\mu\le z\sigma/\sqrt n$. Rearrange: $\bar x_n-z\sigma/\sqrt n\le\mu\le\bar x_n+z\sigma/\sqrt n$. Half-width $z\sigma/\sqrt n\le E\iff n\ge(z\sigma/E)^2=(1.96\cdot20)^2\approx1537$.
