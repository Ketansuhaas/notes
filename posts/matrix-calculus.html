Matrix calculus with differentials

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran

Builds on: Gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians, Four views of matrix multiplication · Leads to: Backpropagation via JVPs

Don't differentiate entry by entry. Nudge the input, keep only the part of the change that's linear in the nudge, and read the gradient off it.

The reading rule

$$ df=\underbrace{\nabla f^T}_{1\times n}\underbrace{dx}_{n\times1} \ \Rightarrow\ \nabla f, \qquad df=\operatorname{tr}\big(G^T\,dX\big)=\textstyle\sum_{ij}G_{ij}\,dX_{ij} \ \Rightarrow\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial X}=G. $$

The gradient with respect to $X$ always has the same shape as $X$.

Toolkit

Product rule$d(XY)=dX\,Y+X\,dY$
Constants pass through$d(AXB)=A\,dX\,B$
A scalar is its own trace$s=\operatorname{tr}(s)$
Trace is cyclic$\operatorname{tr}(ABC)=\operatorname{tr}(CAB)$

Example: $f=\tfrac12x^TAx$, $A$ symmetric

$$df=\tfrac12(dx^TAx+x^TA\,dx)=x^TA\,dx \ \Rightarrow\ \nabla f=Ax.$$

At $(1,1)$ with $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$: $(3,3)$, matching Gradients, Jacobians, and Hessians. ✓

Example: a linear layer

The weight gradient is an outer product: error times input.

$$ L=\tfrac12\|Wx-y\|^2,\ r=Wx-y: \quad dL=r^T\,dW\,x=\operatorname{tr}(x\,r^T\,dW) \ \Rightarrow\ \underbrace{\frac{\partial L}{\partial W}}_{k\times d}=\underbrace{r}_{k\times1}\underbrace{x^T}_{1\times d}. $$

Two you'll meet in papers

$$ d(X^{-1})=-X^{-1}\,dX\,X^{-1}\ \ (\text{differentiate } XX^{-1}=I), \qquad d\log\det X=\operatorname{tr}(X^{-1}dX). $$

Derive it yourself

Least squares: $L(w)=\tfrac12\|Xw-y\|^2$. Find $\nabla L$ and set it to zero.

Solution

$r=Xw-y$, $dL=r^TX\,dw$, so $\nabla L=X^T(Xw-y)$. Zero gives $X^TXw=X^Ty$: the normal equations from Rank, subspaces, and projections.
