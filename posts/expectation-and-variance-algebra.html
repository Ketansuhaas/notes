Expectation and variance algebra

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran

Builds on: Probability notation · Leads to: The covariance matrix, intuitively, Weight initialization, LLN and CLT

Linearity of expectation

Average of a sum = sum of averages. Always, even if the pieces depend on each other.

$$\mathbb{E}[aX+bY+c]=a\,\mathbb{E}X+b\,\mathbb{E}Y+c. \qquad \text{Two dice: } 3.5+3.5=7.$$

Shift and scale

Shifting moves the cloud, it doesn't spread it. Scaling by $a$ stretches distances by $a$, squared distances by $a^2$.

$$\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mu)^2]=\mathbb{E}[X^2]-\mu^2, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}X.$$

Sums

Spreads add, plus a term for moving together. Independent: that term is $0$.

$$\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}X+\operatorname{Var}Y+2\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y).$$

Averages: $\sigma^2/n$

Independent errors partly cancel. Noise of an average shrinks like $1/\sqrt n$.

$$X_i\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}(\mu,\sigma^2): \quad \operatorname{Var}\Big(\textstyle\sum_iX_i\Big)=n\sigma^2, \qquad \operatorname{Var}\Big(\tfrac1n\textstyle\sum_iX_i\Big)=\dfrac{\sigma^2}{n}.$$

Example

Mini-batch of size $B$: gradient noise $\sigma/\sqrt B$. $4\times$ the batch, half the noise.

Vectors: $\operatorname{Cov}(AX)=A\Sigma A^T$

A linear map reshapes the spread, acting from both sides.

$$ \mathbb{E}[AX+b]=A\mu+b, \qquad \underbrace{\operatorname{Cov}(AX+b)}_{m\times m}=\underbrace{A}_{m\times n}\underbrace{\Sigma}_{n\times n}\underbrace{A^T}_{n\times m}. $$

Proof: $AX+b-\mathbb{E}[AX+b]=A(X-\mu)$, so $\mathbb{E}[A(X-\mu)(X-\mu)^TA^T]=A\Sigma A^T$.

Special case

$A=v^T$, a unit row: $\operatorname{Var}(v^TX)=v^T\Sigma v$, the spread along $v$. Next note.

Derive it yourself

$q,k\in\mathbb{R}^d$ independent, entries iid with mean $0$, variance $1$. Show $\operatorname{Var}(q^Tk)=d$. Why does attention divide by $\sqrt d$?

Solution

$q^Tk=\sum_iq_ik_i$. Each term: mean $0$, variance $\mathbb{E}[q_i^2]\,\mathbb{E}[k_i^2]=1$, uncorrelated with the others. Sum: variance $d$. Dividing by $\sqrt d$ brings it back to $1$, so the softmax doesn't saturate as $d$ grows.
