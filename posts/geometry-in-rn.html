Lines, planes, and spheres in $\mathbb{R}^n$

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
linear-algebravectorsgeometry

Builds on: Reading the notation in papers · Leads to: Four views of matrix multiplication, Rank, subspaces, and projections, Quadratic forms and positive definiteness

Three shapes show up everywhere: a line (one free dial), a hyperplane (one constraint), a sphere (one fixed distance). The first two are flat, the third is curved.

Lines

A starting point plus a direction, and one dial $t$ that slides you along it. One free parameter, so a 1-D path.

$$\underbrace{x}_{n\times1}=\underbrace{p}_{n\times1}+t\,\underbrace{d}_{n\times1}, \qquad t\in\mathbb{R},\ d\neq0.$$

Example

$$p=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\ d=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\end{pmatrix} \ \Rightarrow\ x(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1+t\\t\end{pmatrix}, \quad x(0)=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\ x(1)=\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\end{pmatrix}.$$

Hyperplanes

All points with the same dot product against one normal vector $w$. One equation removes one dimension, so it is flat and $(n-1)$-dimensional. A linear classifier's decision boundary $w^Tx+b=0$ is one.

$$w^T(x-p)=0 \iff \underbrace{w^T}_{1\times n}\underbrace{x}_{n\times1}=\underbrace{c}_{1\times1}, \qquad c=w^Tp.$$

$n=2$a line
$n=3$an ordinary plane
$n=784$ (MNIST)a flat 783-dimensional wall

Example

$$p=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix},\ w=\begin{pmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{pmatrix} \ \Rightarrow\ x+y+z=1. \qquad (0,0,1),\ (1,0,0) \text{ both lie on it.}$$

Spheres

All points at distance $r$ from a center $c$. Now $x$ appears twice, so the equation is quadratic: the first curved shape.

$$\|x-c\|=r \iff \underbrace{(x-c)^T}_{1\times n}\underbrace{(x-c)}_{n\times1}=r^2.$$

Example

$c=0,\ r=1$: $x^2+y^2=1$, the unit circle. Check $(1,0)$: $1^2+0^2=1$. ✓

Line$x=p+td$linear, 1 free dial
Hyperplane$w^Tx=c$linear, 1 constraint
Sphere$(x-c)^T(x-c)=r^2$quadratic, curved

Derive it yourself

Show the distance from a point $x_0$ to the hyperplane $w^Tx=c$ is $\dfrac{|c-w^Tx_0|}{\|w\|}$. (Hint: the shortest path runs along the normal, a line $x_0+sw$.)

Solution

Plug the line into the plane: $w^T(x_0+sw)=c \Rightarrow s=\dfrac{c-w^Tx_0}{\|w\|^2}$. The distance travelled is $|s|\,\|w\|=\dfrac{|c-w^Tx_0|}{\|w\|}$. Example: origin to $x+y+z=1$ is $\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$. ✓
