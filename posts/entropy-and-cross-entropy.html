Builds on: Expectation and variance algebra, Convexity and Jensen · Leads to: KL divergence, Supervised learning as KL minimization

Surprise: $-\log p(x)$

Rare events surprise you more. The $\log$ makes surprises of independent events add.

Why $\log$. Ask for a surprise $s(p)$ that is $0$ for a sure thing and adds over independent events, whose probabilities multiply:

$$s(p_1p_2)=s(p_1)+s(p_2).$$

Put $g(t)=s(e^{-t})$; then $g(t_1+t_2)=g(t_1)+g(t_2)$, and a continuous additive $g$ is linear, $g(t)=ct$. With $t=-\ln p$ that is $s(p)=-c\ln p$. Choosing $c$ picks the unit: $\log_2$ gives bits, $\ln$ gives nats ($1$ nat $=1/\ln2\approx1.44$ bits).

$$-\log_21=0, \qquad -\log_2\tfrac12=\log_22=1 \text{ bit}, \qquad -\log_2 2^{-10}=10 \text{ bits}.$$

Entropy: average surprise

How unpredictable a distribution is.

$$H(p)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[-\log p(x)]=-\sum_xp(x)\log p(x).$$

Each row of the table, in bits ($0\log0:=0$, the limit of $p\log p$ as $p\to0$):

$$\text{fair coin: } \tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac12\cdot1=1.$$

$$(0.9,0.1):\ 0.9\cdot\underbrace{(-\log_20.9)}_{0.152}+0.1\cdot\underbrace{(-\log_20.1)}_{3.322}=0.137+0.332=0.47.$$

$$\text{certain: } 1\cdot(-\log_21)=0, \qquad \text{uniform over } K:\ \sum_{x=1}^K\tfrac1K\log K=\log K.$$

Cross-entropy: surprise under wrong beliefs

The world draws from $p$, you expect $q$. Wrong beliefs cost extra surprise.

$$H(p,q)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[-\log q(x)].$$

Example: $p=(0.9,\ 0.1)$, in bits ($-\log_20.9=0.152$, $-\log_20.1=3.322$, $-\log_20.5=1$)

$$q=(0.9,0.1):\ 0.9\cdot0.152+0.1\cdot3.322=0.47 \quad (=H(p)),$$

$$q=(0.5,0.5):\ 0.9\cdot1+0.1\cdot1=1.00,$$

$$q=(0.1,0.9):\ 0.9\cdot3.322+0.1\cdot0.152=2.990+0.015=3.01.$$

The gap is KL

Extra surprise $=$ KL divergence, which is never negative.

Step 1: split the surprise. $-\log q=-\log p+\log\frac pq$ (add and subtract $\log p$). Take $\mathbb{E}_p$ (linearity):

$$H(p,q)=\mathbb{E}_p[-\log p]+\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\log\frac pq\Big]=H(p)+\mathrm{KL}(p\|q).$$

Step 2: $\mathrm{KL}\ge0$. $\log$ is concave, so Jensen gives $\mathbb{E}[\log Y]\le\log\mathbb{E}[Y]$. With $Y=q/p$:

$$-\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)=\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\log\frac qp\Big]\le\log\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\frac qp\Big]$$

$$=\log\sum_{x:\,p(x)\gt0}p(x)\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}=\log\sum_{x:\,p(x)\gt0}q(x)\le\log1=0.$$

Equality needs $q/p$ constant, i.e. $q=p$. So $H(p,q)\ge H(p)$, with equality only for right beliefs.

Check with the table: $\mathrm{KL}(p\|(0.5,0.5))=0.9\log_21.8+0.1\log_20.2=0.763-0.232=0.53$, and $1.00=0.47+0.53$. ✓ For the last row, $\mathrm{KL}=0.9\log_29+0.1\log_2\tfrac19=0.8\log_29=2.54$, and $0.47+2.54=3.01$. ✓

Uniform is the maximum. Take $q=u$, uniform over $K$, so $-\log u(x)=\log K$ for every $x$:

$$\mathrm{KL}(p\|u)=H(p,u)-H(p)=\log K-H(p)\ge0 \ \Rightarrow\ H(p)\le\log K,$$

with equality only at $p=u$. (A direct Jensen proof is the exercise.)

The classification loss

Label $y$ is a one-hot $p$, the model's softmax is $q$. Cross-entropy picks out one term.

True class $k$ means $p=e_k$: $p(j)=1$ if $j=k$, else $0$. Every other term is multiplied by $0$:

$$H(e_k,q)=-\sum_je_k(j)\log q_j=-\log q_k=-\log q_{\text{true class}}.$$

Over a dataset, average this; by Step 1 the average is $H(p_{\text{data}})+\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{data}}\|q)$, and $H(p_{\text{data}})$ has no model parameters in it. So minimizing cross-entropy $=$ minimizing $\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{data}}\|q)$.

Derive it yourself

Show $H(p)\le\log K$ over $K$ outcomes.

Solution

Write entropy as an average of $\log(1/p)$ and apply Jensen ($\log$ concave, so $\mathbb{E}\log Y\le\log\mathbb{E}Y$):

$$H(p)=\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\log\frac1{p(x)}\Big]\le\log\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\frac1{p(x)}\Big]$$

$$=\log\sum_{x:\,p(x)\gt0}p(x)\frac1{p(x)}=\log\#\{x:p(x)\gt0\}\le\log K.$$

Equality in Jensen needs $1/p(x)$ constant on the support, and the last step needs all $K$ outcomes in it: $p$ uniform. Check: the fair coin gives $\log_22=1$ bit. ✓
