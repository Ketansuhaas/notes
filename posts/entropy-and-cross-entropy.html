Entropy and cross-entropy

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
information-theoryentropycross-entropykl-divergenceprobability

Builds on: Expectation and variance algebra, Convexity and Jensen · Leads to: KL divergence, Supervised learning as KL minimization

Surprise: $-\log p(x)$

Rare events surprise you more. The $\log$ makes surprises of independent events add.

$$p=1\to0, \qquad p=\tfrac12\to1 \text{ bit}, \qquad p=\tfrac1{1024}\to10 \text{ bits}; \qquad -\log(p_1p_2)=-\log p_1-\log p_2.$$

$\log_2$ gives bits, $\ln$ gives nats.

Entropy: average surprise

How unpredictable a distribution is.

$$H(p)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[-\log p(x)].$$

Fair coin$1$ bit
Coin $(0.9,0.1)$$0.47$ bits
Certain outcome$0$
Uniform over $K$$\log K$ (the maximum)

Cross-entropy: surprise under wrong beliefs

The world draws from $p$, you expect $q$. Wrong beliefs cost extra surprise.

$$H(p,q)=\mathbb{E}_{x\sim p}[-\log q(x)].$$

Example: $p=(0.9,\ 0.1)$

$q=(0.9,\ 0.1)$$0.47$ bitsright beliefs
$q=(0.5,\ 0.5)$$1.00$ bitno opinion
$q=(0.1,\ 0.9)$$3.01$ bitsconfidently wrong

The gap is KL

Extra surprise $=$ KL divergence, which is never negative (proved via Jensen).

$$ H(p,q)=\mathbb{E}_p[-\log p]+\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\log\frac pq\Big]=H(p)+\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)\ \ge\ H(p). $$

Check: $1.00=0.47+0.53$. ✓

The classification loss

Label $y$ is a one-hot $p$, the model's softmax is $q$. Cross-entropy picks out one term.

$$H(y,q)=-\log q_{\text{true class}}.$$

$H(p_{\text{data}})$ is fixed, so minimizing cross-entropy $=$ minimizing $\mathrm{KL}(p_{\text{data}}\|q)$.

Derive it yourself

Show $H(p)\le\log K$ over $K$ outcomes.

Solution

$H(p)=\mathbb{E}_p\big[\log\tfrac1p\big]\le\log\mathbb{E}_p\big[\tfrac1p\big]=\log\sum_xp(x)\tfrac1{p(x)}=\log K$, by Jensen ($\log$ concave). Equality when $p$ is uniform.
