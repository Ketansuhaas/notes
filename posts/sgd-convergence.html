Stochastic gradient descent: noise and step sizes

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationsgdconvergencenoisestep-size

Builds on: Gradient descent on strongly convex functions, Expectation and variance algebra · Leads to: Adaptive methods: RMSProp and Adam

With noisy gradients, gradient descent's proof goes through with one extra term: the noise. A constant step stalls at a floor proportional to $\eta$. Shrinking $\eta$ removes the floor, but slows everything down.

The noisy gradient

We only see a random $g$ that is right on average, with spread $\sigma^2$.

$$\mathbb E[g\mid x]=\nabla f(x),\qquad \mathbb E\big[\|g-\nabla f(x)\|^2\mid x\big]\le\sigma^2.$$

A mini-batch averages $B$ independent per-example gradients, so its variance is $\sigma^2/B$ (the $\sigma^2/n$ rule for each coordinate, summed).

Key identity. Write $g=\nabla f+(g-\nabla f)$, expand the square, and average (the cross term has mean $0$):

$$\mathbb E\|g\|^2=\|\nabla f\|^2+2\nabla f^T\underbrace{\mathbb E[g-\nabla f]}_{0}+\mathbb E\|g-\nabla f\|^2\le\|\nabla f\|^2+\sigma^2.$$

The noise adds to the squared step size even when $\nabla f=0$.

Goal and two tools

Track the expected gap $\delta_k=\mathbb E[f(x_k)]-f^*$ for $x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta g_k$, exactly as for GD.

Tool 1 (descent lemma, $L$-smooth): $f(y)\le f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)+\tfrac L2\|y-x\|^2$.

Tool 2 (PL inequality, from $\mu$-strong convexity): $\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu\,(f(x)-f^*)$. Derivation: minimize both sides of $f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)+\tfrac\mu2\|y-x\|^2$ over $y$. The right side is smallest at $y=x-\nabla f/\mu$, so

$$f^*\ge f(x)-\frac1\mu\|\nabla f\|^2+\frac1{2\mu}\|\nabla f\|^2=f(x)-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2\mu}.$$

Step 1: one step, in expectation

Same first line as for GD; then average over the noise.

Tool 1 with $y=x_k-\eta g_k$:

$$f(x_{k+1})\le f(x_k)-\eta\,\nabla f(x_k)^Tg_k+\tfrac{L\eta^2}2\|g_k\|^2.$$

Average over $g_k$ with $x_k$ fixed: $\mathbb E[g_k]=\nabla f$ in the middle term, the key identity in the last:

$$\mathbb E[f(x_{k+1})\mid x_k]\le f(x_k)-\eta\|\nabla f\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2}2\big(\|\nabla f\|^2+\sigma^2\big)$$

$$=f(x_k)-\eta\big(1-\tfrac{L\eta}2\big)\|\nabla f\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}2.$$

Take $\eta\le\frac1L$, so $1-\frac{L\eta}2\ge\frac12$:

$$\mathbb E[f(x_{k+1})\mid x_k]\le f(x_k)-\tfrac\eta2\|\nabla f(x_k)\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}2.$$

Step 2: contraction plus noise

Tool 2 turns the gradient term into a fraction of the gap; the noise term stays.

Use $-\tfrac\eta2\|\nabla f\|^2\le-\eta\mu\,(f(x_k)-f^*)$, subtract $f^*$, and average over $x_k$ too (tower rule, $\mathbb E[\mathbb E[\cdot\mid x_k]]=\mathbb E[\cdot]$):

$$\boxed{\ \delta_{k+1}\le(1-\eta\mu)\,\delta_k+\frac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}{2}\ }$$

Check: $\sigma=0$, $\eta=\frac1L$ gives $\delta_{k+1}\le(1-\frac1\kappa)\delta_k$, GD's rate. ✓

Step 3: unroll to a noise floor

The start is forgotten geometrically; the noise kicks pile up into a geometric series with a finite sum.

Apply the box $k$ times:

$$\delta_k\le(1-\eta\mu)^k\delta_0+\frac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}2\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta\mu)^j\le(1-\eta\mu)^k\delta_0+\frac{L\eta^2\sigma^2}2\cdot\frac1{\eta\mu}.$$

$$\delta_k\le\underbrace{(1-\eta\mu)^k\,\delta_0}_{\text{linear, forgets the start}}+\underbrace{\frac{\eta L\sigma^2}{2\mu}}_{\text{noise floor}}.$$

Why a floor: near $x^*$, $\nabla f\approx0$ but $g$ is not, so every step still kicks $x$ by about $\eta\sigma$.

Example

Running example ($L=3$, $\mu=1$), $x_0=(4,0)$ so $\delta_0=16$, noise $\sigma^2=1$ split evenly over the two eigen-directions, $\eta=\frac13$: the bound is $16\,(\tfrac23)^k+0.5$. Exactly, each eigen-coordinate obeys $c\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda)c-\eta\xi$, so

$$\mathbb E[c^2]\leftarrow(1-\eta\lambda)^2\,\mathbb E[c^2]+\tfrac{\eta^2\sigma^2}{2}\ \to\ \frac{\eta^2\sigma^2/2}{1-(1-\eta\lambda)^2}=\begin{cases}\frac{1/18}{1}=0.056&\lambda=3\\[2pt]\frac{1/18}{5/9}=0.1&\lambda=1\end{cases}$$

The true floor is $\tfrac12(3\cdot0.056+1\cdot0.1)=0.133\le0.5$. The bound holds, with room to spare.

Step 4: decaying steps remove the floor

The floor is proportional to $\eta$, so let $\eta$ shrink. With $\eta_k\propto\frac1k$ the gap falls like $\frac1k$.

Take $\eta_k=\frac{2}{\mu(k+k_0)}$ with $k_0=2\kappa$, so $\eta_k\le\eta_0=\frac1L$ and Steps 1 and 2 hold at every step (with $\eta_k$ in place of $\eta$). Claim:

$$\delta_k\le\frac{C}{k+k_0},\qquad C=\max\Big(k_0\delta_0,\ \frac{2L\sigma^2}{\mu^2}\Big).$$

Base $k=0$: $\delta_0=\frac{k_0\delta_0}{k_0}\le\frac C{k_0}$. ✓

Induction. Let $a=k+k_0$ and assume $\delta_k\le\frac Ca$. Since $\eta_k\mu=\frac2a$, the box gives

$$\delta_{k+1}\le\Big(1-\frac2a\Big)\frac Ca+\frac{L\sigma^2}2\cdot\frac4{\mu^2a^2}=\frac Ca-\frac{2C}{a^2}+\frac{2L\sigma^2/\mu^2}{a^2}$$

$$\le\frac Ca-\frac{2C}{a^2}+\frac C{a^2}=\frac{C(a-1)}{a^2}\le\frac{C}{a+1},$$

using $\frac{2L\sigma^2}{\mu^2}\le C$, then $(a-1)(a+1)=a^2-1\le a^2$. ✓ So $\delta_k=O(1/k)$: no floor, but sublinear.

Example

Running example: $k_0=6$, $\eta_k=\frac2{k+6}$, $C=\max(6\cdot16,\ 2\cdot3\cdot1)=96$: $\delta_k\le\frac{96}{k+6}$. At $k=80$ the bound is $1.1$; the exact value is $0.0073$.

Practice combines them: a large constant $\eta$ first, then decay (step or cosine schedules). The floor scales like $\eta\sigma^2/B$, so a bigger batch lowers it the same way a smaller $\eta$ does.

Derive it yourself

Running example: $\delta_0=16$, $L=3$, $\mu=1$, $\sigma^2=1$. Compare $\eta=\frac13$ and $\eta=\frac16$: the floor from Step 3, and the number of steps until the transient $\delta_0(1-\eta\mu)^k$ drops below that floor.

Solution

Floors: $\frac{\eta L\sigma^2}{2\mu}=\frac{3\eta}{2}$, so $0.5$ for $\eta=\frac13$ and $0.25$ for $\eta=\frac16$. Halving $\eta$ halves the floor.

$\eta=\frac13$: $16(\tfrac23)^k\le0.5\iff k\ge\frac{\ln32}{\ln1.5}=\frac{3.466}{0.405}=8.55$, so $9$ steps.

$\eta=\frac16$: $16(\tfrac56)^k\le0.25\iff k\ge\frac{\ln64}{\ln1.2}=\frac{4.159}{0.182}=22.8$, so $23$ steps.

Half the floor costs about $2.6\times$ the steps: twice from the slower rate ($\ln\frac1{1-\eta\mu}\approx\eta\mu$ halves), plus the extra $\ln2$ of distance to the lower floor.
