Smoothness and strong convexity

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationconvexitysmoothnessproofs

Builds on: Convergence rates, Convexity and Jensen, Taylor expansion · Leads to: Gradient descent on smooth convex functions

Two numbers drive every convergence proof: $L$ caps the curvature, $\mu$ floors it. At every point $x$, $f$ is squeezed between a narrow bowl and a wide bowl.

Two bowls

Take the tangent at $x$. Bend it up with curvature $L$: $f$ stays below. Bend it up with curvature $\mu$: $f$ stays above.

$$f(y)\le f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)+\tfrac L2\|y-x\|^2 \qquad \text{(upper bowl)}$$

$$f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)+\tfrac\mu2\|y-x\|^2 \qquad \text{(lower bowl)}$$

Why this $f$ fits $L=3$, $\mu=1$: $(\ln\cosh y)'=\tanh y$ and $(\tanh y)'=1/\cosh^2y\in(0,1]$, so $f''(y)=1+2/\cosh^2y\in(1,3]$.

The definitions, via gradients

$L$-smooth: the gradient never changes faster than $L$ times the distance moved. $\mu$-strongly convex: along any move, the gradient grows at least $\mu$ times the distance.

$$L\text{-smooth:}\quad \|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\le L\|x-y\|.$$

$$\mu\text{-strongly convex:}\quad \big(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\big)^T(x-y)\ge\mu\|x-y\|^2.$$

Deriving the two bowls

Walk the straight line from $x$ to $y$ and add up the slope (fundamental theorem of calculus). The bowls bound how far the gradient can drift from $\nabla f(x)$ along the way.

Step 1: one variable. Let $d=y-x$ and $g(t)=f(x+td)$. By the chain rule $g'(t)=\nabla f(x+td)^Td$, so

$$f(y)-f(x)=g(1)-g(0)=\int_0^1\nabla f(x+td)^Td\,dt.$$

Subtract $\nabla f(x)^Td=\int_0^1\nabla f(x)^Td\,dt$ from both sides:

$$f(y)-f(x)-\nabla f(x)^Td=\int_0^1\big(\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\big)^Td\,dt. \qquad (\ast)$$

Step 2: upper bowl. Cauchy-Schwarz, then smoothness for the points $x+td$ and $x$, which are $t\|d\|$ apart:

$$\big(\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\big)^Td\le\|\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\|\,\|d\|\le Lt\|d\|^2.$$

Put it in $(\ast)$: $\int_0^1Lt\,dt=\tfrac L2$, so the right side of $(\ast)$ is $\le\tfrac L2\|d\|^2$. That is the upper bowl.

Step 3: lower bowl. Strong convexity for the same two points (their difference is $td$), then divide by $t\gt0$:

$$\big(\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\big)^T(td)\ge\mu t^2\|d\|^2$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad \big(\nabla f(x+td)-\nabla f(x)\big)^Td\ge\mu t\|d\|^2.$$

Put it in $(\ast)$: $\int_0^1\mu t\,dt=\tfrac\mu2$, so the right side is $\ge\tfrac\mu2\|d\|^2$. That is the lower bowl.

Hessian version: $\mu I\preceq H\preceq LI$

For twice-differentiable $f$, $L$ and $\mu$ are just the largest and smallest curvature anywhere: bounds on the Hessian's eigenvalues.

Suppose $\mu\|d\|^2\le d^TH(z)d\le L\|d\|^2$ for all $z,d$ (eigenvalues of $H$ in $[\mu,L]$, by the Rayleigh quotient). Taylor with exact remainder, $z$ on the segment from $x$ to $y$:

$$f(y)=f(x)+\nabla f(x)^Td+\tfrac12\underbrace{d^TH(z)d}_{\in[\mu\|d\|^2,\ L\|d\|^2]},$$

which is both bowls at once. Conversely, put $y=x+\epsilon d$. The bowls squeeze the Taylor gap:

$$\tfrac\mu2\epsilon^2\|d\|^2\le\underbrace{f(y)-f(x)-\epsilon\nabla f(x)^Td}_{\frac{\epsilon^2}2d^TH(x)d+o(\epsilon^2)}\le\tfrac L2\epsilon^2\|d\|^2.$$

Divide by $\epsilon^2/2$ and let $\epsilon\to0$: $\mu\|d\|^2\le d^TH(x)d\le L\|d\|^2$.

Running example

$f=x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2=\tfrac12x^TAx$, $H=A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$. $\det(A-\lambda I)=(2-\lambda)^2-1=0$ gives $\lambda=3,1$, so

$$L=3, \qquad \mu=1, \qquad \kappa=\frac L\mu=3.$$

Each bowl is tight in one direction: $d=(1,1)$ gives $d^TAd=2+1+1+2=6=3\|d\|^2$; $d=(1,-1)$ gives $2-1-1+2=2=1\cdot\|d\|^2$.

Consequence 1: the descent lemma

$f$ lies under the upper bowl, so jumping to the bowl's bottom must lower $f$ by at least the bowl's depth.

Completing the square (expand the right side to check), for any $c\gt0$:

$$g^Td+\tfrac c2\|d\|^2=\tfrac c2\Big\|d+\tfrac gc\Big\|^2-\frac{\|g\|^2}{2c}.$$

Upper bowl with $c=L$, $g=\nabla f(x)$:

$$f(y)\le f(x)+\tfrac L2\Big\|y-x+\tfrac{\nabla f(x)}L\Big\|^2-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2L}.$$

The square is $0$ at $y=x-\nabla f(x)/L$, the bowl's bottom. There:

$$\boxed{\ f\Big(x-\tfrac1L\nabla f(x)\Big)\le f(x)-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2L}\ }$$

This is where the step size $\eta=1/L$ comes from.

Example

$x=(1,0)$: $f(x)=1$, $\nabla f=Ax=(2,1)$, $\|\nabla f\|^2=5$. Promise: $f\le1-\tfrac5{6}=\tfrac16$. Actual: $x-\tfrac13(2,1)=(\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$, $f=\tfrac19-\tfrac19+\tfrac19=\tfrac19\le\tfrac16$. ✓

Consequence 2: the PL inequality

$f$ lies above the lower bowl, so $f^*$ is at least the bowl's bottom. If $f(x)$ is far above $f^*$, the gradient at $x$ cannot be small.

Lower bowl with $c=\mu$, same square; then drop the square ($\ge0$):

$$f(y)\ge f(x)+\tfrac\mu2\Big\|y-x+\tfrac{\nabla f(x)}\mu\Big\|^2-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2\mu}$$

$$\ge f(x)-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2\mu}.$$

This holds for every $y$, so also for $y=x^*$. Rearrange:

$$f^*\ge f(x)-\frac{\|\nabla f(x)\|^2}{2\mu} \quad\iff\quad \boxed{\ \|\nabla f(x)\|^2\ge2\mu\big(f(x)-f^*\big)\ }$$

Example

Here $f^*=0$ at $x^*=0$. At $x=(1,0)$: $5\ge2\cdot1\cdot1=2$. ✓ At $x=(1,-1)$: $\nabla f=(1,-1)$, $f=1-1+1=1$, and $2=2\cdot1\cdot1$: tight along the flat direction.

Derive it yourself

Prove the descent lemma directly from the upper bowl by putting in $y=x-\eta\nabla f(x)$. (a) Show $f(y)\le f(x)-\eta\big(1-\tfrac{L\eta}2\big)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2$. (b) Which $\eta$ guarantees the largest decrease? (c) Which $\eta$ guarantee any decrease at all? Compare with the step-size limit in gradient descent on a quadratic.

Solution

(a) Write $g=\nabla f(x)$ and $d=y-x=-\eta g$. Then $g^Td=-\eta\|g\|^2$ and $\tfrac L2\|d\|^2=\tfrac{L\eta^2}2\|g\|^2$. Add them in the upper bowl:

$$f(y)\le f(x)-\eta\|g\|^2+\tfrac{L\eta^2}2\|g\|^2=f(x)-\eta\big(1-\tfrac{L\eta}2\big)\|g\|^2.$$

(b) Maximize $h(\eta)=\eta-\tfrac L2\eta^2$: $h'(\eta)=1-L\eta=0$ at $\eta=\tfrac1L$, where $h=\tfrac1L-\tfrac1{2L}=\tfrac1{2L}$. That is the boxed lemma.

(c) The decrease is positive iff $\eta\big(1-\tfrac{L\eta}2\big)\gt0$ iff $0\lt\eta\lt\tfrac2L$. On a quadratic $L=\lambda_{\max}$, so this is exactly the limit $\eta\lt2/\lambda_{\max}$, now for any $L$-smooth $f$.
