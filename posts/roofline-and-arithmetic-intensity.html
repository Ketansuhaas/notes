Arithmetic intensity and the roofline

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
gpurooflinearithmetic-intensitymatmulperformance

Builds on: Inside a GPU: SMs, warps, and memory, Four views of matrix multiplication · Leads to: The CUDA programming model

Every kernel needs math and bytes. Whichever takes longer sets the speed, and one ratio tells you which.

Arithmetic intensity

How much math you do per byte fetched from HBM. Low intensity: the lanes starve, waiting for data.

$$I=\frac{\text{FLOPs}}{\text{bytes moved to and from HBM}}.$$

Example: ReLU in bf16

Per element: 1 FLOP (a max), read 2 B, write 2 B.

$$I_{\text{bf16}}=\frac{1}{2+2}=0.25\ \text{FLOP/B},\qquad I_{\text{fp32}}=\frac{1}{4+4}=0.125\ \text{FLOP/B}.$$

Elementwise. A fixed $c$ FLOPs per element, one read and one write of $s$ bytes each:

$$I=\frac{cN}{2N\cdot s}=\frac{c}{2s}\quad\text{(no }N\text{: bigger tensors never help).}$$

Matmul. $C=AB$ has $n^2$ entries, each a dot product $C_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^nA_{ik}B_{kj}$ of $n$ multiplies and $n$ adds:

$$\text{FLOPs}=\underbrace{n^2}_{\text{outputs}}\times\underbrace{2n}_{\text{per output}}=2n^3.$$

$$\text{bytes (each matrix touched once)}=\underbrace{3n^2}_{A,\,B,\,C}\cdot s \quad\Rightarrow\quad I=\frac{2n^3}{3n^2s}=\frac{2n}{3s}.$$

The FLOPs grow one power of $n$ faster than the data, because each value is used $n$ times.

The roofline

Two pipes, and the narrower one wins. Below some intensity, bandwidth caps you; above it, peak math does.

Step 1: time for each pipe. A kernel does $F$ FLOPs and moves $Q$ bytes. The lanes alone need $F/P_{\text{peak}}$; the memory alone needs $Q/B$ ($B$ = HBM bandwidth). The GPU overlaps them (other warps compute while some wait, see latency hiding), so the slower one sets the time:

$$t=\max\!\Big(\frac{F}{P_{\text{peak}}},\ \frac{Q}{B}\Big).$$

Step 2: divide. Attained rate is $F/t$. Dividing $F$ by a max is the min of the quotients, and $F/Q=I$:

$$\frac{F}{t}=\min\!\Big(\frac{F}{F/P_{\text{peak}}},\ \frac{F}{Q/B}\Big)=\min\big(P_{\text{peak}},\ B\cdot I\big).$$

Step 3: the ridge. The two terms are equal where $B\cdot I^\ast=P_{\text{peak}}$:

$$\boxed{\ \text{attainable FLOP/s}=\min\big(P_{\text{peak}},\ B\cdot I\big)\ }\qquad \boxed{\ I^\ast=\frac{P_{\text{peak}}}{B}\ }\ \text{(ridge)}$$

On log-log axes $\log(B\cdot I)=\log B+\log I$ is a line of slope 1, and $P_{\text{peak}}$ is flat: the roof shape.

Example: ReLU on an A100

$I=0.25\lt156$, so the bandwidth term wins the min:

$$B\cdot I=2\times10^{12}\times0.25=5\times10^{11}=0.5\ \text{TFLOP/s},\qquad \frac{0.5}{312}\approx0.16\%\text{ of peak}.$$

Its runtime is the $Q/B$ term of Step 1: for $N$ elements, $t=4N/B$. The math is free.

What to do about it

Read the fix off the formula: each side of the min has its own knobs.

$$I\lt I^\ast:\quad \text{rate}=B\cdot\frac{F}{Q}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{lower }Q\text{ (or raise }B).$$

$$I\gt I^\ast:\quad \text{rate}=P_{\text{peak}}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{raise }P_{\text{peak}}.$$

memory-bound ($I\lt I^\ast$)move fewer bytes: fuse ops, use smaller dtypes, reuse data from shared memory
compute-bound ($I\gt I^\ast$)do math faster: tensor cores, lower-precision math

Work it out yourself

An $(n\times n)(n\times n)$ fp16 matmul reads $A$ and $B$ once and writes $C$ once. Find $I(n)$. At what $n$ does it cross the A100 ridge? The H100 ridge? And a matrix-vector product?

Solution

FLOPs: $n^2$ outputs $\times$ $2n$ each. Bytes: three $n\times n$ matrices at $2$ B per value.

$$I(n)=\frac{2n^3}{3n^2\cdot2}=\frac{2n^3}{6n^2}=\frac n3\ \text{FLOP/B}.$$

Set $I(n)=I^\ast$ and solve:

$$\text{A100: }\frac n3=156\Rightarrow n=468\approx470,\qquad \text{H100: }\frac n3=296\Rightarrow n\approx890.$$

LLM layers ($n$ in the thousands) are compute-bound.

Matrix-vector $y=Ax$: $n$ outputs $\times$ $2n$ FLOPs; bytes are dominated by the matrix ($n^2$ values) since $x,y$ are only $2n$ values:

$$I=\frac{2n^2}{(n^2+2n)\cdot2}\approx\frac{2n^2}{2n^2}=1\ \text{FLOP/B}\ \ll156.$$

Deeply memory-bound. Each matrix entry is used once, not $n$ times. This is why generating one token at a time is limited by bandwidth.
