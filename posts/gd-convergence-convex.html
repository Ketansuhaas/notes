Gradient descent on smooth convex functions

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationgradient-descentconvergenceproofs

Builds on: Smoothness and strong convexity, Convexity and Jensen · Leads to: Gradient descent on strongly convex functions

Knowing only "convex" and "$L$-smooth", gradient descent still always gets there, at the sublinear rate $1/k$. The proof is a template worth owning: find a per-step inequality whose right side is a difference, then sum and let it telescope.

The claim

Step size $1/L$, any convex $L$-smooth $f$ with a minimizer $x^*$. No strong convexity needed.

$$x_{k+1}=x_k-\tfrac1L\nabla f(x_k) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad f(x_k)-f^*\le\frac{L\,\|x_0-x^*\|^2}{2k}.$$

Shorthand: $g_k=\nabla f(x_k)$, $\ \text{gap}_k=f(x_k)-f^*$, $\ D_k=\|x_k-x^*\|^2$.

The plan

Neither tool alone works: the descent lemma knows nothing about $x^*$, and convexity knows nothing about the step. Combine them, and aim for a right side of the form $D_k-D_{k+1}$, because a sum of differences collapses.

Step 1: the two ingredients

One inequality says how much $f$ drops; the other says how far $f$ is above $f^*$. Both are in terms of $g_k$.

Descent lemma (previous note) at $x_k$:

$$f(x_{k+1})\le f(x_k)-\frac{\|g_k\|^2}{2L}.$$

Convexity: the tangent at $x_k$ lies below $f$, in particular at $x^*$ (tangent test):

$$f^*\ge f(x_k)+g_k^T(x^*-x_k) \iff f(x_k)\le f^*+g_k^T(x_k-x^*).$$

Substitute the second into the first and subtract $f^*$:

$$\text{gap}_{k+1}\le g_k^T(x_k-x^*)-\frac{\|g_k\|^2}{2L}. \qquad (1)$$

Step 2: the key inequality

The right side of (1) has a cross term and a square term, the fingerprint of an expanded $\|a-b\|^2$. The square it came from is the distance to $x^*$ after one step.

$x_{k+1}-x^*=(x_k-x^*)-\tfrac1Lg_k$. Expand with $\|a-b\|^2=\|a\|^2-2a^Tb+\|b\|^2$:

$$D_{k+1}=D_k-\tfrac2L\,g_k^T(x_k-x^*)+\tfrac1{L^2}\|g_k\|^2.$$

Multiply by $\tfrac L2$ and move $D_{k+1}$ to the other side:

$$\tfrac L2\big(D_k-D_{k+1}\big)=g_k^T(x_k-x^*)-\frac{\|g_k\|^2}{2L},$$

which is exactly the right side of (1). So

$$\boxed{\ \text{gap}_{k+1}\le\tfrac L2\big(D_k-D_{k+1}\big)\ }$$

Bonus: $\text{gap}_{k+1}\ge0$, so $D_{k+1}\le D_k$. Every step ends at least as close to $x^*$.

Step 3: sum and telescope

Add the boxed line for $k=0,\dots,K-1$. Each $D$ in the middle appears once with $+$ and once with $-$.

$$\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{gap}_k\le\tfrac L2\big[(D_0-D_1)+(D_1-D_2)+\cdots+(D_{K-1}-D_K)\big]$$

$$=\tfrac L2\big(D_0-D_K\big)\le\tfrac L2D_0 \qquad (D_K\ge0).$$

Step 4: last $\le$ average

The sum bounds the average gap. Since $f$ never goes up, the last gap is the smallest, so it is below the average.

$f$ never rises: $f(x_{k+1})\le f(x_k)-\tfrac{\|g_k\|^2}{2L}\le f(x_k)$. So $\text{gap}_K\le\text{gap}_k$ for every $k\le K$, and

$$K\cdot\text{gap}_K\le\sum_{k=1}^{K}\text{gap}_k\le\tfrac L2D_0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{gap}_K\le\frac{L\,D_0}{2K}. \qquad\blacksquare$$

What the rate means

This is sublinear, $O(1/k)$: by convergence rates, accuracy $\varepsilon$ needs $k\ge LD_0/(2\varepsilon)$, and each extra digit costs 10 times the steps. It never used $\mu$, so it covers flat-bottomed problems ($\mu=0$), like least squares with more features than samples.

Running example

$f=x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2$, $L=3$, $x^*=0$, $x_0=(1,0)$, so $D_0=1$ and the bound is $\tfrac3{2k}$. One step: $x_1=x_0-\tfrac13(2,1)=(\tfrac13,-\tfrac13)$, so $D_1=\tfrac29$ and $\text{gap}_1=\tfrac19-\tfrac19+\tfrac19=\tfrac19$. Key inequality:

$$\tfrac19\le\tfrac32\big(1-\tfrac29\big)=\tfrac32\cdot\tfrac79=\tfrac76. \ \checkmark$$

Derive it yourself

Show that $f(x_k)$ is non-increasing along gradient descent with step $1/L$, and that it strictly decreases unless $x_k$ is already a minimizer.

Solution

Descent lemma, then $\|g_k\|^2\ge0$ and $L\gt0$:

$$f(x_{k+1})\le f(x_k)-\frac{\|g_k\|^2}{2L}\le f(x_k).$$

Chaining over $k$ gives $f(x_0)\ge f(x_1)\ge f(x_2)\ge\cdots$. If $g_k\ne0$, the subtracted term is $\gt0$, so the drop is strict. If $g_k=0$, the tangent test gives $f(y)\ge f(x_k)+0^T(y-x_k)=f(x_k)$ for all $y$: $x_k$ is already a global minimizer. (Measured above: $1\gt0.111\gt0.049\gt\cdots$.)
