Law of large numbers and the central limit theorem

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran

Builds on: Expectation and variance algebra, Concentration inequalities · Leads to: Sample variance and degrees of freedom, Confidence intervals

The sample mean is random

A different dataset gives a different $\bar x_n$. Its spread shrinks like $1/\sqrt n$, and that one fact drives both theorems below.

$$x_i\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}(\mu,\sigma^2), \qquad \bar x_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nx_i, \qquad \mathbb{E}[\bar x_n]=\mu, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\bar x_n)=\frac{\sigma^2}n.$$

Three kinds of convergence

Random sequences can "converge" in stronger or weaker senses.

$X_n\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}X$$P(\lim_nX_n=X)=1$each path settles
$X_n\xrightarrow{p}X$$P(|X_n-X|\gt\epsilon)\to0\ \ \forall\epsilon$big gaps get rare
$X_n\xrightarrow{d}X$$P(X_n\le x)\to P(X\le x)$only the histogram settles

$$\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\ \Rightarrow\ \xrightarrow{p}\ \Rightarrow\ \xrightarrow{d} \quad(\text{not the reverse}).$$

Law of large numbers

Averaging cancels noise. With enough samples, $\bar x_n$ has nowhere to go but $\mu$.

$$\text{Weak: } \bar x_n\xrightarrow{p}\mu. \qquad \text{Chebyshev: } P(|\bar x_n-\mu|\ge\epsilon)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon^2}\to0.$$

$$\text{Strong: } \bar x_n\xrightarrow{\text{a.s.}}\mu \quad(\text{needs only a finite mean; proof omitted}).$$

Example: fair coin, $\sigma^2=\tfrac14$

$n=10{,}000$, $\epsilon=0.05$: $P(|\bar x_n-0.5|\ge0.05)\le\dfrac{0.25}{10^4\cdot0.0025}=0.01$.

Central limit theorem

LLN says where $\bar x_n$ ends up. CLT zooms in on the error at the $\sqrt n$ scale: it always looks Gaussian, whatever $p$ is (if $\sigma^2\lt\infty$).

$$\sqrt n\,(\bar x_n-\mu)\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2) \quad\iff\quad Z_n=\frac{\bar x_n-\mu}{\sigma/\sqrt n}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal N(0,1).$$

So for large $n$: $\bar x_n\approx\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2/n)$. Proof (via characteristic functions) omitted.

Example: fair coin, $n=100$

$\bar x_n\approx\mathcal N(0.5,\ 0.05^2)$, so $95\%$ of the time $\bar x_n\in0.5\pm1.96\cdot0.05\approx[0.40,\ 0.60]$.

Where it shows up

Confidence intervals$\bar x_n\pm1.96\,\sigma/\sqrt n$
Monte Carloerror of $\frac1n\sum f(x_i)$ is $\approx$ Gaussian, size $\sigma/\sqrt n$
Minibatch SGDgradient noise $\propto1/\sqrt B$, often modeled as Gaussian

Derive it yourself

Show that $\sqrt n$ is the only scaling $n^a$ for which $n^a(\bar x_n-\mu)$ has a nontrivial limit: compute its variance and see what happens for $a\lt\tfrac12$ and $a\gt\tfrac12$.

Solution

$\operatorname{Var}\big(n^a(\bar x_n-\mu)\big)=n^{2a}\cdot\frac{\sigma^2}n=n^{2a-1}\sigma^2$. For $a\lt\tfrac12$ this $\to0$ (the error collapses to a point, the LLN view). For $a\gt\tfrac12$ it $\to\infty$. Only $a=\tfrac12$ keeps variance $\sigma^2$, the CLT scale.
