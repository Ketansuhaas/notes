The reparameterization trick

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
deep-learninggenerative-modelingreparameterization-trickgradients

Builds on: Generative modeling, Automatic differentiation, Expectation and variance algebra · Leads to: VAEs and diffusion (coming)

A VAE's loss averages over random samples whose distribution depends on the parameters. Move the randomness into fixed noise, and the gradient flows straight through the sample.

The problem

$\theta$ lives in the distribution, not in $f$. A sampled $z$ is just a number: autodiff sees no path from it back to $\theta$.

$$ \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim q_\theta}[f(z)]=\int f(z)\,\nabla_\theta q_\theta(z)\,dz \quad\ne\quad \mathbb{E}_{z\sim q_\theta}[\nabla_\theta f(z)]=0. $$

The left integral is not an average under $q_\theta$, so sampling $z$ and averaging doesn't estimate it.

The trick: $z=\mu+\sigma\epsilon$

Write the sample as a deterministic function of $\theta$ and noise with no $\theta$ in it. The randomness moves to $\epsilon$; $\theta$ moves into the function.

$$z=\mu+\sigma\epsilon,\quad \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1) \quad\Rightarrow\quad z\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2), \qquad \theta=(\mu,\sigma).$$

$$ \nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}_{z\sim q_\theta}[f(z)]=\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}[f(\mu+\sigma\epsilon)]=\mathbb{E}_{\epsilon}\big[\nabla_\theta f(\mu+\sigma\epsilon)\big]\approx\frac1S\sum_{s=1}^S\nabla_\theta f(\mu+\sigma\epsilon_s). $$

Chain rule through the sample: $\ \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\mu}=f'(z),\quad \dfrac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma}=f'(z)\,\epsilon$.

In a VAE the encoder outputs $\mu,\sigma$: $\ \underbrace{z}_{d\times1}=\underbrace{\mu_\phi(x)}_{d\times1}+\underbrace{\sigma_\phi(x)}_{d\times1}\odot\underbrace{\epsilon}_{d\times1}$.

General recipe: any $z=g_\theta(\epsilon)$ with $\epsilon\sim p(\epsilon)$ free of $\theta$.

$\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$$z=\mu+\sigma\epsilon$, $\ \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,1)$
$\mathcal N(\mu,\Sigma)$, $\Sigma=LL^T$$z=\mu+L\epsilon$, $\ \epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$
$\text{Exponential}(\lambda)$$z=-\tfrac1\lambda\log u$, $\ u\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$

Example: $f(z)=z^2$

$$\text{Exact: } \mathbb{E}[z^2]=\mu^2+\sigma^2\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}=2\mu. \qquad \text{Pathwise: } \frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}(\mu+\sigma\epsilon)^2=2(\mu+\sigma\epsilon),\ \ \text{mean } 2\mu.$$

One sample at $\mu=1,\ \sigma=0.5,\ \epsilon=0.4$: estimate $2.4$, true $2$. Noisy, but unbiased. ✓

The alternative: score function (REINFORCE)

Without a reparameterization, differentiate the density instead, using $\nabla q=q\,\nabla\log q$.

$$\nabla_\theta\,\mathbb{E}_{q_\theta}[f(z)]=\mathbb{E}_{z\sim q_\theta}\big[f(z)\,\nabla_\theta\log q_\theta(z)\big].$$

Derive it yourself

Same example: $f(z)=z^2$, $z=\mu+\sigma\epsilon$. Find the pathwise estimator of $\partial\,\mathbb{E}[f]/\partial\sigma$ and show it is unbiased.

Solution

$\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}(\mu+\sigma\epsilon)^2=2(\mu+\sigma\epsilon)\,\epsilon$. Mean: $2\mu\,\mathbb{E}[\epsilon]+2\sigma\,\mathbb{E}[\epsilon^2]=0+2\sigma$. Exact: $\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}(\mu^2+\sigma^2)=2\sigma$. ✓
