Gradient descent on a quadratic

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationgradient-descenteigenvalueshessiancondition-number

Builds on: Taylor expansion, Dynamics in the eigenbasis, Quadratic forms · Leads to: momentum and Adam (coming)

Near a minimum every loss is a bowl (Taylor). On a bowl, gradient descent splits into independent 1-D problems, one per eigenvector of the curvature.

The update

Shift the minimum to the origin and the bowl is $\tfrac12x^TAx$. A gradient step is then multiplication by one fixed matrix.

Why a bowl: near a minimum $x^\star$, $\nabla f(x^\star)=0$, so Taylor leaves $f(x^\star+e)\approx f(x^\star)+\tfrac12e^THe$. Rename $e\to x$, $H\to A$, drop the constant, and take the gradient as in matrix calculus ($A$ symmetric):

$$f(x)=\tfrac12x^TAx,\qquad \nabla f=Ax.$$

$$x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\nabla f(x_k)=x_k-\eta Ax_k=(I-\eta A)\,x_k.$$

Running example: $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, so $f=x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2$. Its eigenvalues solve $\det(A-\lambda I)=(2-\lambda)^2-1=0$, so $2-\lambda=\pm1$:

$$\lambda_1=3:\ (A-3I)q=0\Rightarrow q_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1), \qquad \lambda_2=1:\ (A-I)q=0\Rightarrow q_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1).$$

One factor per direction

$I-\eta A$ has the same eigenvectors as $A$. Each coordinate gets multiplied by its own factor every step.

Same eigenvectors. Apply $I-\eta A$ to an eigenvector, $Aq_i=\lambda_iq_i$:

$$(I-\eta A)q_i=q_i-\eta\lambda_iq_i=(1-\eta\lambda_i)\,q_i.$$

Decouple. $A$ is symmetric, so its $q_i$ form an orthonormal basis (spectral theorem). Write $x_k=\sum_ic_i^{(k)}q_i$ and apply the update term by term (linearity):

$$x_{k+1}=\sum_ic_i^{(k)}(1-\eta\lambda_i)\,q_i \quad\Rightarrow\quad c_i^{(k+1)}=(1-\eta\lambda_i)\,c_i^{(k)}.$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad c_i^{(k)}=(1-\eta\lambda_i)^k\,c_i^{(0)}.$$

Each factor is $1-\eta\lambda_i$ with $\lambda_1=3$, $\lambda_2=1$:

$\eta$steep $q_1$: $1-3\eta$flat $q_2$: $1-\eta$behaviour
$0.2$$1-0.6=0.4$$0.8$smooth, slow along $q_2$
$0.5$$1-1.5=-0.5$$0.5$zig-zags along $q_1$ (sign flips)
$0.7$$1-2.1=-1.1$$0.3$diverges along $q_1$ ($|{-1.1}|^k\to\infty$)

Example: the right panel below, $\eta=0.5$ from $(4,0)$

$$I-0.5A=\begin{pmatrix}1-1&-0.5\\-0.5&1-1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-0.5\\-0.5&0\end{pmatrix}.$$

Each step swaps the coordinates and multiplies by $-0.5$:

$$(4,0)\to(0,-2)\to(1,0)\to(0,-0.5)\to\cdots$$

The step-size limit

Every factor must shrink, and the steepest direction breaks first.

From $c_i^{(k)}=(1-\eta\lambda_i)^kc_i^{(0)}$, coordinate $i$ goes to $0$ from every start iff $|1-\eta\lambda_i|\lt1$. Unpack it (with $\lambda_i\gt0$):

$$-1\lt1-\eta\lambda_i\lt1 \iff 0\lt\eta\lambda_i\lt2 \iff 0\lt\eta\lt\frac2{\lambda_i}.$$

This must hold for every $i$, and $2/\lambda_i$ is smallest for the largest $\lambda$:

$$|1-\eta\lambda_i|\lt1\ \ \forall i \iff 0\lt\eta\lt\frac2{\lambda_{\max}}.$$

Here $\lambda_{\max}=3$, so $\eta\lt\tfrac23\approx0.667$: the table's $\eta=0.7$ is just past it.

The condition number sets the speed

$\eta$ must be small for the steep direction, which leaves the flat direction crawling. The ratio $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ decides how bad that is.

The overall rate. The $q_i$ are orthonormal, so lengths add like Pythagoras. Let $\rho(\eta)=\max_i|1-\eta\lambda_i|$:

$$\|x_k\|^2=\sum_i(1-\eta\lambda_i)^{2k}\big(c_i^{(0)}\big)^2\le\rho^{2k}\|x_0\|^2.$$

So $\|x_k\|\le\rho^k\|x_0\|$, with equality when $x_0$ lies along the worst direction. $1-\eta\lambda$ is a straight line in $\lambda$, so its largest $|\cdot|$ over $[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]$ is at an end:

$$\rho(\eta)=\max\big(|1-\eta\lambda_{\min}|,\ |1-\eta\lambda_{\max}|\big).$$

Best fixed step: balance the two ends. Raising $\eta$ shrinks the flat term $1-\eta\lambda_{\min}$ but, past $1/\lambda_{\max}$, grows the steep term $\eta\lambda_{\max}-1$. The max is smallest where they meet:

$$1-\eta\lambda_{\min}=-(1-\eta\lambda_{\max}) \iff 2=\eta(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max}).$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad \eta^\star=\frac2{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}}.$$

Any other $\eta$ makes one of the two terms bigger. Plug back in, then divide top and bottom by $\lambda_{\min}$:

$$\rho^\star=1-\eta^\star\lambda_{\min}=\frac{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}-2\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}}$$

$$=\frac{\lambda_{\max}-\lambda_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}}=\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}.$$

Here: $\eta^\star=\tfrac2{3+1}=0.5$ and $\rho^\star=\tfrac{3-1}{3+1}=0.5$, matching the table row $\eta=0.5$ (factors $-0.5$ and $0.5$, equal size).

Steps per digit. One more correct digit means shrinking the error $10\times$: $\rho^k=0.1$, so

$$k=\frac{\ln10}{-\ln\rho}=\frac{2.303}{\ln\frac{\kappa+1}{\kappa-1}}.$$

$$\kappa=3:\ \ \rho=0.5,\ \ k=\frac{2.303}{\ln2}=\frac{2.303}{0.693}\approx3.3.$$

$$\kappa=100:\ \ \rho=\tfrac{99}{101}\approx0.980,\ \ k=\frac{2.303}{\ln\frac{101}{99}}=\frac{2.303}{0.0200}\approx115.$$

For large $\kappa$, $\ln\frac{1+1/\kappa}{1-1/\kappa}\approx\frac2\kappa$, so $k\approx1.15\,\kappa$: steps grow linearly with the condition number.

$\kappa=3$ (here)rate $0.5$about 3 steps per digit
$\kappa=100$rate $0.98$about 115 steps per digit

This is why momentum, Adam, normalization and good initialization exist: they fight a large $\kappa$.

Derive it yourself

Precondition: $x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\,PAx_k$ with $P=A^{-1}$ and $\eta=1$. What happens, and what is the new $\kappa$?

Solution

The update matrix is now $I-\eta PA$. With $P=A^{-1}$, $\eta=1$:

$$x_{k+1}=(I-A^{-1}A)\,x_k=(I-I)\,x_k=0.$$

The minimum in one step, from any start. The matrix playing the role of $A$ is $PA=I$: every eigenvalue is $1$, so $\kappa=1/1=1$, $\rho^\star=\tfrac{1-1}{1+1}=0$.

This is Newton's method: the step $-A^{-1}\nabla f=-A^{-1}Ax$ is exactly $-H^{-1}\nabla f$ from Taylor expansion. Adam's per-coordinate scaling is a cheap diagonal stand-in for $P$.
