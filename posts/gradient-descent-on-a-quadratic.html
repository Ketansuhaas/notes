Gradient descent on a quadratic

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
optimizationgradient-descenteigenvalueshessiancondition-number

Builds on: Taylor expansion, Dynamics in the eigenbasis, Quadratic forms · Leads to: momentum and Adam (coming)

Near a minimum every loss is a bowl (Taylor). On a bowl, gradient descent splits into independent 1-D problems, one per eigenvector of the curvature.

The update

$$f(x)=\tfrac12x^TAx,\quad \nabla f=Ax \quad\Rightarrow\quad x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta Ax_k=(I-\eta A)\,x_k.$$

Running example: $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, so $f=x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2$, with $\lambda_1=3$ along $q_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ and $\lambda_2=1$ along $q_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$.

One factor per direction

$I-\eta A$ has the same eigenvectors as $A$. Each coordinate gets multiplied by its own factor every step.

$$x=\textstyle\sum_ic_iq_i: \qquad c_i\ \leftarrow\ (1-\eta\lambda_i)\,c_i.$$

$\eta$steep $q_1$: $1-3\eta$flat $q_2$: $1-\eta$behaviour
$0.2$$0.4$$0.8$smooth, slow along $q_2$
$0.5$$-0.5$$0.5$zig-zags along $q_1$
$0.7$$-1.1$$0.3$diverges along $q_1$

The step-size limit

Every factor must shrink, and the steepest direction breaks first.

$$|1-\eta\lambda_i|\lt1\ \ \forall i \iff 0\lt\eta\lt\frac2{\lambda_{\max}}. \qquad \text{Here: } \eta\lt\tfrac23.$$

The condition number sets the speed

$\eta$ must be small for the steep direction, which leaves the flat direction crawling. The ratio $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$ decides how bad that is.

$$\text{best } \eta=\frac2{\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min}}, \qquad \text{rate}=\frac{\kappa-1}{\kappa+1}.$$

$\kappa=3$ (here)rate $0.5$about 3 steps per digit
$\kappa=100$rate $0.98$about 115 steps per digit

This is why momentum, Adam, normalization and good initialization exist: they fight a large $\kappa$.

Derive it yourself

Precondition: $x_{k+1}=x_k-\eta\,PAx_k$ with $P=A^{-1}$ and $\eta=1$. What happens, and what is the new $\kappa$?

Solution

$x_{k+1}=(I-A^{-1}A)x_k=0$: the minimum in one step. Effectively $PA=I$, all eigenvalues $1$, so $\kappa=1$. This is Newton's method; Adam is a cheap diagonal approximation of $P$.
