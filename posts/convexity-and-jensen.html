Convexity and Jensen's inequality

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran

Builds on: Taylor expansion, Expectation and variance algebra · Leads to: Entropy and cross-entropy, Primal and dual problems

Convex = bowl-shaped

Any chord lies above the graph; any tangent lies below it.

Chord$f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\le\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)$, $\ \lambda\in[0,1]$
Tangent$f(y)\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)$
Curvature$H(x)\succeq0$ everywhere (every direction curves up)

The chord test is the definition. For smooth $f$ the three are equivalent; each arrow is a few lines.

Why the three tests agree

A chord above the graph, squeezed to zero length, becomes a tangent below it. Tangents below the graph average into a chord above it. And the tangent's error is exactly the curvature term of Taylor.

Chord $\Rightarrow$ tangent. Write the chord point as $x+\lambda(y-x)$ and rearrange the chord inequality:

$$f(x+\lambda(y-x))\le f(x)+\lambda\big(f(y)-f(x)\big)$$

$$\Rightarrow\quad \frac{f(x+\lambda(y-x))-f(x)}{\lambda}\le f(y)-f(x).$$

Let $\lambda\to0$: the left side is the slope of $f$ along $y-x$, which is $\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)$ (gradient note). That is the tangent test.

Tangent $\Rightarrow$ chord. Let $z=\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y$ and use the tangent at $z$ twice:

$$f(x)\ge f(z)+\nabla f(z)^T(x-z), \qquad f(y)\ge f(z)+\nabla f(z)^T(y-z).$$

Multiply by $\lambda$ and $1-\lambda$ and add. The gradient terms cancel because

$$\lambda(x-z)+(1-\lambda)(y-z)=\underbrace{\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y}_{z}-z=0,$$

leaving $\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)\ge f(z)$: the chord test.

Curvature $\Rightarrow$ tangent. Taylor with exact remainder, for some $z$ on the segment from $x$ to $y$:

$$f(y)=f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x)+\tfrac12\underbrace{(y-x)^TH(z)(y-x)}_{\ge0\text{ since }H(z)\succeq0}$$

$$\ge f(x)+\nabla f(x)^T(y-x).$$

Tangent $\Rightarrow$ curvature. Put $y=x+\epsilon v$ into the tangent test, and expand $f(y)$ to second order:

$$0\le f(x+\epsilon v)-f(x)-\epsilon\nabla f^Tv=\tfrac12\epsilon^2\,v^TH(x)v+O(\epsilon^3).$$

Divide by $\epsilon^2$ and let $\epsilon\to0$: $v^TH(x)v\ge0$ for every $v$.

Examples

Convex, by the curvature test in 1-D ($f''\ge0$):

$$(x^2)''=2, \qquad (e^x)''=e^x\gt0, \qquad (-\log x)''=\Big(-\frac1x\Big)'=\frac1{x^2}\gt0.$$

$\|x\|$ has a kink at $0$, so use the chord test directly (triangle inequality, then $\|cx\|=|c|\|x\|$):

$$\|\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\|\le\lambda\|x\|+(1-\lambda)\|y\|.$$

$x_1^2+x_1x_2+x_2^2$: $H=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}$, and $\det(H-\mu I)=(2-\mu)^2-1=0$ gives eigenvalues $3,1\ge0$.

Not convex: $(\sin x)''=-\sin x\lt0$ on $(0,\pi)$. A neural-network loss: swapping two hidden units (and their weights) gives the same loss, so a minimum $w$ has a twin $w'$ with equal loss; the midpoint $\tfrac12(w+w')$ averages the two units into identical copies and is typically worse, breaking the chord test.

Local is global

The tangent is a floor under the whole function. A flat tangent means nothing anywhere is lower.

Put $\nabla f(x)=0$ into the tangent test:

$$f(y)\ge f(x)+0^T(y-x)=f(x) \ \ \text{for every } y.$$

So any stationary point of a convex function is a global minimum.

Jensen's inequality

For a bowl, the average of outputs beats the output of the average.

$$f \text{ convex}: \quad f(\mathbb{E}X)\le\mathbb{E}f(X).$$

Proof. Tangent at $\mu=\mathbb{E}X$, holding for every value of $X$:

$$f(X)\ge f(\mu)+f'(\mu)(X-\mu).$$

Take $\mathbb{E}$ of both sides; by linearity $\mathbb{E}[X-\mu]=\mathbb{E}X-\mu=0$:

$$\mathbb{E}f(X)\ge f(\mu)+f'(\mu)\cdot0=f(\mathbb{E}X).$$

Concave version. $\log$ is concave because $-\log$ is convex (above). Jensen for $-\log$, then multiply by $-1$:

$$-\log\mathbb{E}X\le\mathbb{E}[-\log X] \quad\Rightarrow\quad \log\mathbb{E}X\ge\mathbb{E}\log X.$$

Example

$f(x)=x^2$: Jensen says $(\mathbb{E}X)^2\le\mathbb{E}X^2$, i.e. $\operatorname{Var}X=\mathbb{E}X^2-(\mathbb{E}X)^2\ge0$. In the figure, $X=-1$ or $2$ with probability $\tfrac12$ each:

$$\mathbb{E}X=\tfrac12(-1+2)=0.5, \qquad f(\mathbb{E}X)=0.25,$$

$$\mathbb{E}f(X)=\tfrac12(1+4)=2.5, \qquad \text{gap}=2.5-0.25=2.25.$$

And directly $\operatorname{Var}X=\tfrac12\big((-1.5)^2+1.5^2\big)=2.25$. ✓

Derive it yourself

Prove $\mathrm{KL}(p\|q)=\sum_xp(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\ge0$.

Solution

Flip the fraction to put $-\mathrm{KL}$ in the form $\mathbb{E}\log(\cdot)$, with $X=q(x)/p(x)$ and $x\sim p$:

$$-\mathrm{KL}=\sum_xp(x)\log\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}=\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\log\frac qp\Big].$$

Concave Jensen, $\mathbb{E}\log X\le\log\mathbb{E}X$:

$$\le\log\mathbb{E}_p\Big[\frac qp\Big]=\log\sum_{x:\,p(x)\gt0}p(x)\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}=\log\sum_{x:\,p(x)\gt0}q(x).$$

That sum is at most $\sum_xq(x)=1$, and $\log1=0$. So $-\mathrm{KL}\le0$.
