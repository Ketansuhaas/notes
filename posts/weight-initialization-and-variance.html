Weight initialization and variance propagation

Ketan Suhaas Saichandran
deep-learningvarianceweight-initialization

Builds on: Expectation and variance algebra, Backpropagation via JVPs, Dynamics in the eigenbasis · Leads to: normalization layers (coming)

Every layer multiplies the signal's variance by the same factor. Depth raises that factor to the power $L$, so initialization sets it to $1$.

One layer: factor $n_{\text{in}}\sigma_W^2$

Each output sums $n_{\text{in}}$ zero-mean, uncorrelated terms, so their variances add.

$$ \underbrace{y}_{n_{\text{out}}\times1}=\underbrace{W}_{n_{\text{out}}\times n_{\text{in}}}\underbrace{x}_{n_{\text{in}}\times1},\quad W_{ji}\overset{\text{iid}}{\sim}(0,\sigma_W^2) \ \Rightarrow\ \operatorname{Var}(y_j)=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{in}}}\mathbb{E}[W_{ji}^2]\,\mathbb{E}[x_i^2]=n_{\text{in}}\,\sigma_W^2\,\mathbb{E}[x^2]. $$

Needs $W$ independent of $x$. For zero-mean $x$: $\mathbb{E}[x^2]=\operatorname{Var}(x)$.

Depth: the factor to the power $L$

As in Dynamics in the eigenbasis: repeated multiplication explodes above $1$ and dies below $1$.

$$\operatorname{Var}(h_L)=(n_{\text{in}}\sigma_W^2)^L\operatorname{Var}(x) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \text{LeCun: } \sigma_W^2=\frac1{n_{\text{in}}}.$$

Example: $n_{\text{in}}=512$, $10$ linear layers

$\sigma_W=1$factor $512$$512^{10}\approx10^{27}$: explodes
$\sigma_W=0.01$factor $0.05$$\approx10^{-13}$: vanishes
$\sigma_W^2=1/512$factor $1$$1$: stable

Backward pass: Xavier

Gradients flow back through $W$ from the other side, summing over $n_{\text{out}}$ terms. One $\sigma_W^2$ can't fix both directions unless $n_{\text{in}}=n_{\text{out}}$, so average.

$$ \underbrace{g_x}_{1\times n_{\text{in}}}=\underbrace{g_y}_{1\times n_{\text{out}}}\underbrace{W}_{n_{\text{out}}\times n_{\text{in}}} \ \Rightarrow\ \operatorname{Var}(g_x)=n_{\text{out}}\,\sigma_W^2\operatorname{Var}(g_y). $$

Forward stable$\sigma_W^2=1/n_{\text{in}}$LeCun
Backward stable$\sigma_W^2=1/n_{\text{out}}$
Compromise$\sigma_W^2=2/(n_{\text{in}}+n_{\text{out}})$Xavier/Glorot

Example

$256\to1024$: LeCun $1/256$, Xavier $2/1280=1/640$.

ReLU halves the second moment: He

ReLU zeros the negative half. For a symmetric pre-activation, the kept half carries exactly half of $\mathbb{E}[z^2]$, so double $\sigma_W^2$.

$$ \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{ReLU}(z)^2]=\mathbb{E}[z^2\,\mathbf 1_{z\gt0}]=\tfrac12\mathbb{E}[z^2] \ \Rightarrow\ \operatorname{Var}(z_{\ell+1})=n_{\text{in}}\sigma_W^2\cdot\tfrac12\operatorname{Var}(z_\ell) \ \Rightarrow\ \sigma_W^2=\frac2{n_{\text{in}}}. $$

ReLU outputs aren't zero-mean, which is why the layer rule uses $\mathbb{E}[x^2]$: only the second moment enters.

Example

$n_{\text{in}}=512$: He $\sigma_W=\sqrt{2/512}=0.0625$, LeCun $\sigma_W\approx0.044$.

Derive it yourself

Frameworks often sample $W_{ji}\sim\mathcal U(-a,a)$. Find the $a$ that gives the Xavier variance $2/(n_{\text{in}}+n_{\text{out}})$.

Solution

$\operatorname{Var}(W_{ji})=\mathbb{E}[W_{ji}^2]=\int_{-a}^a\frac{w^2}{2a}\,dw=\frac{a^2}3$. Set $\frac{a^2}3=\frac2{n_{\text{in}}+n_{\text{out}}}$: $\ a=\sqrt{\frac6{n_{\text{in}}+n_{\text{out}}}}$, the formula in the Glorot paper. He-uniform likewise gives $a=\sqrt{6/n_{\text{in}}}$.
